# A0.6 Putting It Together & A0 Exam (Alles samen)

## Unit at a glance

This lesson (about 7 hours) brings all of A0 together. You'll add two last everyday tools, **telling the time** and **the days of the week**, learn a step-by-step method for reading any Dutch word aloud, and then practise every sentence pattern you know in one big workshop. ## Part 2: Step 1: Telling the time

### Full hours

| Dutch | English |
| --- | --- |
| Hoe laat is het? | What time is it? |
| Het is drie uur. | It's three o'clock. |
| Het is twaalf uur. | It's twelve o'clock. |
| om acht uur | at eight o'clock |
| Hoe laat ...? | At what time ...? |

**Rule:** "o'clock" is **uur**, and it never gets an -s: **drie uur**, not "drie uren".

**Rule:** "at" a time is **om**: **Om negen uur werk ik.** (At nine I work. Verb second!)

### Half: the big trap

**Careful:** Dutch counts the half hour **towards the next hour**. **half vier** (literally "half four") means **3.30**, not 4.30. Think of it as "half on the way to four".

| Clock | Dutch | Think |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.30 | half vier | halfway to four |
| 7.30 | half acht | halfway to eight |
| 12.30 | half een | halfway to one |
| 9.30 | half tien | halfway to ten |

### Quarters and minutes

| Clock | Dutch | Literally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.15 | kwart over drie | quarter past three |
| 3.45 | kwart voor vier | quarter to four |
| 3.10 | tien over drie | ten past three |
| 3.50 | tien voor vier | ten to four |
| 3.05 | vijf over drie | five past three |
| 3.55 | vijf voor vier | five to four |

**Pattern:** **over** = past, **voor** = to. Up to quarter past, count from the hour. After quarter to, count to the next hour.

**Note:** between quarter past and quarter to, Dutch counts around the half: 3.20 is **tien voor half vier** (ten before half four) and 3.40 is **tien over half vier** (ten after half four). It takes practice. For now, it's enough to understand it. You'll practise it fully in A1.7.

**Note:** timetables, shops and official letters use the 24-hour clock: **15.30** is said **vijftien uur dertig**.

## Part 4: Step 3: Reading any word aloud

### What you need to know

Dutch spelling is regular, so you can read words you've never seen. Use these five steps every time.

**Step 1:** split the word into syllables. Put the break before a single consonant (**wo-nen**, **ma-ken**), and between two consonants (**wer-ken**, **zit-ten**).

**Step 2:** for each syllable, decide open or closed. Open (ends in a vowel) = long. Closed with a single vowel = short. Double vowels (aa, ee, oo, uu) and ie, oe, ui, ij, ei, ou, au, eu are always as you learned them.

**Step 3:** put the stress on the first syllable, unless the word starts with **be-, ge-, ver-** or **ont-**. Those are never stressed: be-TA-len (to pay), ver-KO-per (seller).

**Step 4:** say unstressed **e** as a schwa ("uh"), especially in **-en, -el, -er, -e** at the end: **lo-pen**, **ta-fel**, **va-der**.

**Step 5:** final **d** = t, final **b** = p, **g/ch** = the rough sound, **-ig** = "-ukh".

### Worked examples

| Word | Syllables | How to say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| de molen (windmill) | mo-len | long o, schwa: MOH-luh(n) |
| de bakker (baker) | bak-ker | short a, schwa: BAK-kur |
| betalen (to pay) | be-ta-len | stress on ta, long a: buh-TAH-luh(n) |
| de vogel (bird) | vo-gel | long o, rough g, schwa: VOH-khul |
| de straten (streets) | stra-ten | long a: STRAH-tuh(n) |

>
