# A0.4 Having and Doing: hebben and Regular Verbs (Ik woon, ik werk, ik heb)

## Unit at a glance

This lesson (about 7 hours) is the engine room of Dutch. You learn **hebben** (to have), the numbers 0 to 20, and the system that every regular Dutch verb follows. After it you can say where you live, what you do, which languages you speak and what you have, about yourself and anyone else. You'll also learn **geen** ("no", "not a"), so you can say what you **don't** have.

**You will be able to:**

- Use every form of **hebben**: ik heb, jij hebt, hij heeft, wij hebben...
- Count from 0 to 20 and say how many you have and how old someone is
- Use spelling rule 2 (open and closed syllables) to spell any verb form
- Make the present tense of any regular verb: ik woon, jij woont, wij wonen
- Choose between **niet** and **geen**
- Say final d and b correctly (hond = "hont"), and tell v/f and z/s apart

**Builds on:** A0.1 to A0.3 (spelling rule 1, pronouns, zijn, de/het/een, niet).

## Part 1: The big picture

You know **zijn** (to be). Today you learn the second key verb, **hebben** (to have), and then the pattern behind almost all other verbs.

**Key idea:** most Dutch verbs follow one simple system in the present tense. There are only **three forms** to learn for each verb:

| Who | Form | wonen (to live) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ik | the stem | ik woon |
| jij, u, hij, zij, het | stem + t | jij woont, hij woont |
| wij, jullie, zij | the full verb | wij wonen |

English has two forms ("I live", "he lives"). Dutch has three. Learn this table and you can use hundreds of verbs.

## Part 2: Step 1: hebben, to have

### What you need to know

**hebben** is irregular, like zijn, but only a little.

| Pronoun | hebben | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ik | heb | I have |
| jij / je | hebt | you have |
| u | heeft (or hebt) | you have (formal) |
| hij / zij / ze / het | heeft | he / she / it has |
| wij / we | hebben | we have |
| jullie | hebben | you have (plural) |
| zij / ze | hebben | they have |

**Pattern:** same as zijn: all plurals use the full verb (**hebben**). Only **hij/zij/het** has a surprise: **heeft**, not "hebt".

**Rule:** **u heeft** is the most common formal form. **u hebt** is also correct. Pick one and stick with it.

## Part 4: Step 3: Spelling rule 2, open and closed syllables

### What you need to know

You need this rule for the verbs in Step 4. Dutch words are split into syllables (beats): **wo-nen**, **wer-ken**, **zit-ten**.

- A syllable is **closed** when it ends in a consonant: **wer-**, **zit-**, **man**.
- A syllable is **open** when it ends in a vowel: **wo-**, **ma-**, **le-**.

**Rule 2:** in an **open** syllable, **one** vowel letter is enough for a **long** sound. **wo-nen** has a long o, written with one o. **ma-ken** has a long a, written with one a.

So the long sound is spelled **double** when the syllable is closed (rule 1: **woon**, **maak**), but **single** when it is open (rule 2: **wo-nen**, **ma-ken**).

| Closed (rule 1: double) | Open (rule 2: single) | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ik woon | wij wo-nen | live |
| ik maak | wij ma-ken | make |
| ik spreek | wij spre-ken | speak |
| ik leer | wij le-ren | learn |
| de boom | de bo-men | tree, trees |

### Keeping a short vowel short: double the consonant

If the vowel is short and the word gets an extra syllable, Dutch doubles the consonant to keep the first syllable closed: **zit** → **zit-ten**, **kat** → **kat-ten**.

| Short vowel | With extra syllable | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ik zit | wij zit-ten | sit |
| ik bak | wij bak-ken | bake |
| de man | de man-nen | man, men |
| de bom | de bom-men | bomb, bombs |

**Why this matters:** **bomen** (trees) and **bommen** (bombs) differ only in this rule. And in Step 4, it tells you how to spell every verb.

## Part 5: Step 4: How every regular verb works

### What you need to know

Every Dutch verb has a full form (the **infinitive**), which is the form in the dictionary. It almost always ends in **-en**: **wonen, werken, spreken**.

**Rule:** to find the **stem**, take **-en** off the infinitive, then fix the spelling with rules 1 and 2.

**Pattern:** ik + stem. jij / u / hij / zij / het + stem + **t**. wij / jullie / zij + infinitive.

### Worked example: werken (to work)

| Who | Form | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ik | werk | I work |
| jij / u | werkt | you work |
| hij / zij / het | werkt | he / she / it works |
| wij / jullie / zij | werken | we / you / they work |

### Four spelling fixes for the stem

**Step A: take off -en.** werk-en → **werk**. Done, no fix needed.

**Step B: keep a long vowel long.** wo-nen → won? No: in **won** the o would be short (rule 1). Write it double: **woon**. Same for spreken → **spreek**, maken → **maak**, leren → **leer**.

**Step C: one consonant at the end, not two.** zit-ten → zitt? Dutch never ends a word in a double consonant: **zit**. Same for bakken → **bak**.

**Step D: v becomes f, z becomes s.** Dutch words never end in v or z. leven (to live, be alive) → **leef**. lezen (to read) → **lees**.

**Careful:** if the stem already ends in **-t**, don't add another t. **zitten** → ik zit, hij **zit** (not "zitt"). **praten** → ik praat, hij **praat**. **eten** → ik eet, hij **eet**. **heten** → ik heet, hij **heet**.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| wonen | woon | woont | to live (somewhere) |
| werken | werk | werkt | to work |
| spreken | spreek | spreekt | to speak |

| komen | kom | komt | to come |
| heten | heet | heet | to be called |
| maken | maak | maakt | to make |
| koken | kook | kookt | to cook |
| drinken | drink | drinkt | to drink |

| lezen | lees | leest | to read |
| zitten | zit | zit | to sit |
| fietsen | fiets | fietst | to cycle |
| praten | praat | praat | to talk |

**Note:** **komen** has a short o: ik **kom**, not "koom". It's one of a few verbs where the vowel is short in the stem but long in the infinitive. Just learn it.

**Note:** a few very short verbs (**gaan, doen, zien, staan**) work a little differently. You'll learn them in A1.3.

## Part 6: Step 5: niet or geen?

### What you need to know

Dutch has two words for "not". You already know **niet**. The second one is **geen**, which means "no" or "not a/any" in front of a noun.

**Rule:** use **geen** in front of a noun that has **een** or **no article** in the positive sentence. Use **niet** everywhere else.

| Positive | Negative | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ik heb een auto. | Ik heb **geen** auto. | een + noun → geen |
| Ik drink koffie. | Ik drink **geen** koffie. | noun without article → geen |
| Ik heb tijd. | Ik heb **geen** tijd. | noun without article → geen |
| Hij spreekt Nederlands. | Hij spreekt **geen** Nederlands. | language without article → geen |
| Ik ben student. | Ik ben **geen** student. | job without article → geen |
| Ik werk. | Ik werk **niet**. | a verb → niet |
| Het huis is groot. | Het huis is **niet** groot. | describing word → niet |
| Ik woon in Utrecht. | Ik woon **niet** in Utrecht. | a place → niet |
| Dat is de sleutel. | Dat is **niet** de sleutel. | de/het + noun → niet |

**Pattern:** think of **geen** as "not + een" melted together: **niet een** → **geen**.

### Where niet goes

With the verbs in this lesson, **niet** goes:

- at the **end** of a short sentence: **Ik werk niet.** **Hij kookt niet.**
- **before** a describing word or a place: **Ik woon niet in Utrecht.** **Ik ben niet moe.**

You'll learn the full rules for longer sentences in A1.4.

## Part 7: Sounds: final d and b, v/f, z/s

### Final d and b sound like t and p

At the end of a word, **d** is said as **t**, and **b** as **p**. The spelling keeps the d and b because they come back in longer forms.

| Written | Sounds like | Longer form (d/b come back) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| de hond | "hont" | de honden |
| goed | "khoot" | goede |
| het kind | "kint" | de kinderen |
| ik heb | "ik hep" | wij hebben |

**Careful:** English speakers say a soft "d" at the end ("hond" like "fond"). Dutch ears hear that as an accent. Make it a clean, crisp **t**.

### v/f and z/s

**v** and **f** are close in Dutch, and so are **z** and **s**. In the Netherlands, many speakers say v almost like f, and z almost like s. Aim for a slightly softer sound for v and z, but don't worry too much: nobody will misunderstand you.

| Example | English |
| --- | --- |
| vier, vijf | four, five |
| zes, zeven | six, seven |
| ik lees, wij lezen | I read, we read |
| ik leef, wij leven | I live, we live |
