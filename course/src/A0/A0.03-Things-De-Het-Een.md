# A0.3 Things: de, het and een (Dit is een huis)

## Unit at a glance

So far you've talked about people. In this lesson (about 6 hours) you start talking about things. You'll learn why Dutch has two words for "the" (**de** and **het**), how to say "a" (**een**), how to point at things (**Dit is een fiets**), how to describe them (**De fiets is oud**) and how to say "not" (**De koffie is niet warm**). You'll also learn the four Dutch "double" vowel sounds: **ui, ij/ei, ou/au** and **eu**.

**You will be able to:**

- Use **de** and **het** correctly with 30 everyday nouns, and use five rules that predict which one a noun takes
- Use **een** (a) and tell it apart from **één** (one)
- Point at things with **dit is / dat is**
- Describe things with zijn + a describing word, and join two ideas with **en** and **maar**
- Make sentences negative with **niet**
- Say the sounds **ui, ij/ei, ou/au** and **eu**

**Builds on:** A0.1 (spelling rules, sentence pattern) and A0.2 (pronouns, zijn, questions with zijn).

## Part 1: The big picture

English has one word for "the". Dutch has two: **de** and **het**. Every Dutch noun belongs to one of two groups:

- **de-words**, like **de man**, **de fiets** (bike), **de koffie**
- **het-words**, like **het huis**, **het boek**, **het water**

There's no logic in most cases: it's like a family name that each noun carries. But it matters, because later grammar (describing words in A1.8, "this/that" in A1.2) depends on it.

**Key idea:** never learn a noun on its own. Learn **de fiets**, not "fiets". From now on, every noun in this course is shown with its de or het, and the word cards are coloured: **blue edge = de-word**, **orange edge = het-word**.

**Good news:** about two out of three nouns are de-words, and all plurals are de-words. So if you really don't know, **de** is the better guess.

### Five rules that help

You can't predict every noun, but these five rules cover a lot of them. Learn the rules, and learn the rest one by one.

**Rule 1:** words for people are **de**-words: de man, de vrouw, de docent, de student. (Exception to learn: **het kind**, **het meisje**.)

**Rule 2:** **all plurals** are de-words: het boek, but **de** boeken (the books). You'll learn plurals in A1.2.

**Rule 3:** small-thing words ending in **-je** are **het**-words: het meisje (girl), het kopje (cup), het huisje (little house). The **-je** ending means "little", and it always takes het.

**Rule 4:** words ending in **-ing** are **de**-words: de woning (home), de rekening (bill), de vergadering (meeting).

**Rule 5:** languages are **het**-words: het Nederlands, het Engels. "Dutch is easy" is **Het Nederlands is makkelijk**.

## Part 3: Step 2: een, "a"

### What you need to know

The Dutch word for "a" or "an" is **een**. It's the same for de-words and het-words.

| the | a |
| --- | --- |
| de fiets | een fiets |
| het huis | een huis |
| de appel | een appel |
| het boek | een boek |

**Rule:** **een** is pronounced with a schwa: "un", short and quick. It's not stressed.

**Careful:** when **een** means the number **one**, it's stressed and pronounced with a long ee ("ayn"). In writing it then often gets accents: **één**. **Een fiets** = a bike. **Eén fiets** or **één fiets** = one bike (not two).

**Remember:** after **zijn**, a job takes no een: **Ik ben docent**. But a thing does: **Dit is een fiets**.

7. a key ___ [[een sleutel]]
8. the book ___ [[het boek]]

## Part 4: Step 3: Pointing, dit is / dat is

### What you need to know

**Dit** means "this" (near you). **Dat** means "that" (further away). With **is**, they work for every noun, de or het:

**Pattern:** DIT IS / DAT IS + een or de/het + noun.

| Dutch | English |
| --- | --- |
| Dit is een fiets. | This is a bike. |
| Dat is een huis. | That is a house. |
| Dit is de sleutel. | This is the key. |
| Dat is het station. | That is the station. |
| Wat is dat? | What's that? |
| Dat is een tas. | That's a bag. |

**New word:** **wat** means "what". **Wat is dat?** (What's that?) is one of the most useful questions you can learn: point at anything and ask.

**Note:** for people you already know **Dat is Pieter** from A0.2. It's the same pattern.

### Practice: build the sentence

1. This is a bike. {{fiets / is / Dit / een}} [[Dit is een fiets.]]

3. What's that? {{is / Wat / dat}} [[Wat is dat?]]
4. This is the key. {{de / Dit / sleutel / is}} [[Dit is de sleutel.]]
5. That is a child. {{kind / is / een / Dat}} [[Dat is een kind.]]

### Practice: answer the question

## Part 5: Step 4: Describing things

### What you need to know

You already used **zijn** to say how a person is: **Ik ben moe**. Use the same pattern for things.

**Pattern:** THING + IS + DESCRIBING WORD. **De fiets is oud.** (The bike is old.)

**Rule:** after **zijn**, the describing word never changes. It's the same for de-words, het-words, one thing or many: **De fiets is oud. Het huis is oud.**

(Before a noun it can get an extra -e, as in "the old bike", **de oude fiets**. That's for A1.8. For now, always put the describing word after the verb.)

| Dutch | English |
| --- | --- |
| groot | big |
| klein | small |
| oud | old |
| nieuw | new |
| mooi | beautiful, nice-looking |
| lelijk | ugly |
| lekker | tasty, nice (food, weather) |
| warm | warm, hot |
| koud | cold |
| duur | expensive |
| goedkoop | cheap |
| leuk | nice, fun |
| open | open |
| dicht | closed, shut |
| schoon | clean |

### Worked examples

| Dutch | English |
| --- | --- |
| Het huis is groot. | The house is big. |
| De keuken is klein. | The kitchen is small. |
| De koffie is warm. | The coffee is hot. |
| Het brood is lekker. | The bread is tasty. |
| De auto is duur. | The car is expensive. |
| De deur is open. | The door is open. |

### Joining two ideas: en and maar

**en** means "and" and **maar** means "but". They join two sentences without changing the word order of either one.

| Dutch | English |
| --- | --- |
| Het huis is oud en het is mooi. | The house is old and it's beautiful. |
| De auto is nieuw, maar hij is lelijk. | The car is new, but it's ugly. |
| De koffie is lekker en warm. | The coffee is tasty and hot. |

**Note:** look at the second example. When you talk about a **de**-thing again, Dutch usually says **hij** (literally "he"), not **het**: **De auto ... hij is lelijk.** For a **het**-thing you say **het**: **Het huis ... het is mooi.** You'll hear this all the time, so just notice it now.

3. The bike is new. {{De / nieuw / fiets / is}} [[De fiets is nieuw.]]

5. The car is cheap but ugly. {{goedkoop / De auto / maar / is / lelijk}} [[De auto is goedkoop maar lelijk.|De auto is goedkoop, maar lelijk.]]

## Part 6: Step 5: Saying "not" with niet

### What you need to know

**niet** means "not". In a sentence with **zijn**, it comes right after the verb, before the describing word or the place.

**Pattern:** PERSON/THING + ZIJN + NIET + describing word or place.

| Positive | Negative | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ik ben moe. | Ik ben niet moe. | I'm not tired. |
| Het huis is groot. | Het huis is niet groot. | The house isn't big. |
| De koffie is warm. | De koffie is niet warm. | The coffee isn't hot. |
| Hij is thuis. | Hij is niet thuis. | He isn't at home. |
| Dat is de sleutel. | Dat is niet de sleutel. | That isn't the key. |

**Rule:** with zijn, **niet** goes straight after the verb.

**Careful:** for a job, Dutch doesn't use niet. "I'm not a student" is **Ik ben geen student**. You'll learn **geen** (no, not a) in A0.4.

### Answering "no" fully

Now you can answer a yes/no question properly:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Ben je moe? | Nee, ik ben niet moe. |
| Is de koffie warm? | Nee, de koffie is niet warm. |
| Is het huis duur? | Nee, het is niet duur. |
| Is Pieter thuis? | Nee, hij is niet thuis. |

## Part 7: Sounds: ui, ij/ei, ou/au, eu

These four sounds are spelled with two letters but are one sound each (a "gliding" vowel). Three of them don't exist in English, so they need careful practice.

| Spelling | How to make it | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ui | Start with a rounded "uh", then glide towards uu with your lips still rounded. Not English "ow" or "wee". | het huis, de tuin, uit |
| ij / ei | Between English "ay" (day) and "eye". Start with an open mouth. ij and ei sound exactly the same. | het ijs, klein, de trein |
| ou / au | Like "ow" in "how", but start with a rounder mouth. ou and au sound the same. | oud, koud, de vrouw |
| eu | Say the Dutch long ee, then round your lips. No glide. Like French "peu". | leuk, de deur, de keuken |

**Note:** **ij** and **ei** sound the same, and so do **ou** and **au**. You just have to learn which spelling each word uses: **klein** but **wijn** (wine), **oud** but **blauw** (blue).

**Careful:** **ij** is often written as one letter, and at the start of a sentence both letters get a capital: **IJsland** (Iceland), **IJs is lekker**.
