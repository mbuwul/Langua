# Dutch Course Blueprint (A0 to C1)

## Overview

The course takes an English speaker from zero Dutch to C1 in six levels (A0 to C1), with about 70 units and a mock certification exam at the end of every level. Finishing it should mean you can pass the Inburgeringsexamen, Staatsexamen NT2 I and II, CNaVT and ITNA, work in Dutch, and read research papers.

- **Standard taught:** Standard Dutch as spoken in the Netherlands (Algemeen Nederlands), with Flemish pronunciation and vocabulary introduced for listening from A2.
- **Explanations in English** up to A2, mixed from B1, and mostly in Dutch from B2.
- **Four skills every unit:** listening, speaking, reading and writing. Pronunciation and vocabulary review run alongside.
- **Spaced review:** every new word and grammar point comes back on a schedule, so the course remembers what you are about to forget.
- **Built to reuse:** levels, units and exam projects are a template. Another language swaps in its own content and exams.

## How every unit is built

Each unit takes about 8 to 12 hours. Every unit has the same eight parts, so learners always know what comes next.

1. **Situation:** a short video or audio scene, for example ordering at a bakery. It sets the goal: "By the end you can do this."
2. **Words:** 40 to 80 new words and phrases with native audio, pictures and an example sentence. Each word goes into spaced review.
3. **Grammar:** one or two points explained in plain English with colour-coded examples, then practised in drills.
4. **Pronunciation:** one sound or rhythm feature. You listen, record yourself, and the app scores you and shows what to fix.
5. **Listening & reading:** an authentic-style text or recording with comprehension questions in the same format as the exams.
6. **Speaking:** a role-play with an AI conversation partner, which corrects your mistakes and explains them afterwards.
7. **Writing:** a task for that level, such as a form, a message, an email or an essay. It's marked against the exam criteria with written feedback.
8. **Culture & review:** a short note on Dutch or Flemish life, then a 15-minute quiz mixing this unit with earlier ones.

You can only move on when you pass the unit quiz with 80% or more. Every fourth unit ends with a **checkpoint** that mixes all four skills.

### A0 lessons are built differently

A complete beginner can't learn from a dialogue full of unknown words. So the six A0 lessons teach first and use later. Each one has:

1. **The big picture:** what this lesson adds to your map of Dutch, and why it matters.
2. **Steps (three to five):** each step is one idea, taught in order: a plain-English explanation, a **rule box**, worked examples, then guided practice that builds sentences.
3. **Sounds:** the pronunciation focus, with mouth instructions and a microphone check.
4. **Your words:** every word of the lesson, after you've already used it.
5. **Listen & read:** a short text that uses only what's been taught (new words are glossed).
6. **Speak, Write, Review:** the four skills and a quiz mixing all earlier A0 lessons.

**Rule for the whole course:** no unit uses a word or rule in an exercise before it has been taught. Chunks needed early (like "Hoe gaat het?") are labelled as fixed phrases.

**Practice types used from A0 on:** type-in answers with instant marking, word tiles to build sentences in the right order, and a free microphone check in the browser (Chrome or Edge) that shows which words came through clearly. Every exercise block shows your score as you go.

## A0 and A1: foundations and everyday survival

A0 teaches how Dutch works: its sounds and spelling, and the skeleton of every sentence (pronouns, zijn, hebben, regular verbs, de/het, niet/geen, verb second, questions). It's taught step by step with rule boxes and intensive sentence-building practice. A1 then uses that skeleton in the situations of your first months in the Netherlands, and adds new grammar on top.

## Pronunciation track

Pronunciation is practised in every unit and gets harder as you go, from single sounds to fluent connected speech. The app plays the model, records you, and gives feedback on each sound and on rhythm.

- **A0 (individual sounds):** short and long vowels, the diphthongs ui, ij/ei, ou/au, eu, oe, plus g/ch, sch, w, the r, and the final -t sound at the end of words like hond.
- **A1 (words):** word stress, the unstressed "e" (schwa), diminutives, spelling aloud.
- **A2 (phrases):** weak forms (’t, ‘k, ze), linking words together, question and statement intonation.
- **B1 (sentences):** rhythm of long sentences, sounds changing between words, polite vs direct tone.
- **B2 (natural speech):** modal-particle intonation, fast casual speech, listening to many regional accents.
- **C1 (public speaking):** presentation pacing, emphasis, and controlling your accent in formal settings.

Known problem sounds for English speakers get extra drills: ui, g/ch, short a vs long aa, the Dutch r, and not pronouncing the final d as a d.

## Word-building track

Knowing how Dutch builds words lets you guess thousands of words you have never seen. From A2 on, the Words part of every unit ends with a short word-building section.

- **A0 to A1 (spelling and small words):** open and closed syllables (bomen, bommen), plurals with -en and -s, diminutives with -je, -tje, -pje, -etje.
- **A2 (compounds):** joining nouns (de fiets + de winkel = de fietsenwinkel), which word decides de or het, the linking -s- and -e(n)- (stadhuis, boekenkast).
- **B1 (prefixes and suffixes):** be-, ver-, ont-, her-, on- (betalen, vergeten, ontvangen, herhalen, onmogelijk); nouns with -ing, -heid, -te, -schap (de opleiding, de vrijheid, de lengte, de vriendschap); adjectives with -lijk, -ig, -baar, -loos.
- **B2 (word families):** turning verbs into nouns for formal writing (invoeren, de invoering), separable and inseparable verbs with the same root (voorkomen vs voorkómen), people words with -er, -aar, -ist, -ster.
- **C1 (academic words):** Latin and Greek roots shared with English, word families in the Academische Woordenlijst, choosing the formal or informal member of a family.

## Exam projects

Each level ends with a project built like the real exam it prepares for. It uses the same four skills, task types and timing, plus a marking guide based on the official criteria. Passing at 70% or more on every part means you're ready to book the real exam. Formats below are from memory and will be checked against the official exam guides when I write the projects.

| Level | Real exam it mirrors | Who needs it | What the project covers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A0 | None (course check) | Everyone | Reading aloud with scored pronunciation, reading, grammar and sentence building, writing, speaking |
| A1 | Basisexamen Inburgering Buitenland | Partners applying to move to the Netherlands | Speaking, reading, and Dutch society basics |
| A2 | Inburgeringsexamen (A2 route) and CNaVT A2 | Newcomers on the A2 route; holiday and informal use | Reading, listening, writing, speaking, KNM practice |
| B1 | Inburgeringsexamen (B1 route), Staatsexamen NT2 Programma I, CNaVT B1, ITNA B1 | Residents, work in practical jobs, Flemish colleges | Four separate parts with timed computer-style tasks and recorded speaking |
| B2 | Staatsexamen NT2 Programma II, CNaVT B2 (professional and higher-education profiles), ITNA B2 | University study and professional jobs | Four parts at exam length, plus an oral interview for ITNA |
| C1 | CNaVT C1 | Senior professional and academic roles | Exam simulation plus capstone (below) |

**C1 capstone:** read a Dutch research paper in your field and write a 1,000-word critical summary. Then give a 15-minute presentation with questions, and do a mock job interview, all in Dutch. You finish the course when the capstone passes.

Each project can be retaken with new questions, and the app shows which skill held you back.

## Decisions (approved 7 Nov 2026)

- Netherlands Dutch as the main standard, with Flemish used for listening practice
- Explanations in English until A2, then gradually in Dutch
- C1 as the final level (C2 can be added later)
- 20 Nov 2026: A0 rebuilt as six foundation lessons (structure first, then words), after a beginner tester found the old start too thin on rules and too focused on repeating words
- Learners choose a track at C1: Academic or Professional
