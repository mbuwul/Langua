# A1.2 Family & People (Familie en mensen)

## Unit at a glance

After this unit (about 9 hours) you can talk about your family, show someone a photo, and say who is who. You'll learn the two Dutch words for "the" (**de** and **het**), the word **een**, how to make plurals, and how to say "my", "your" and "our". In pronunciation you meet the most common sound in Dutch: the weak "uh" sound called the **schwa**.

**You will be able to:**

- Name about 40 family members and people words, each with de or het
- Make plurals with -en, -s and 's, and spot the common irregular ones
- Say whose something is: mijn, je, uw, zijn, haar, ons/onze, jullie, hun
- Point at people with dit, dat, deze and die
- Hear and say the schwa in words like de, vader, kinderen and gezellig
- Congratulate people at a Dutch birthday party the Dutch way

**Builds on:** A0.3 (de, het, een), A0.4 (open and closed syllables) and A1.1.

## Part 1: Situation

### Dialogue 1: Een foto van de familie (A family photo)

Kofi meets Sanne, his new **taalmaatje** (language buddy). He shows her a photo on his phone. They use **je**.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sanne | Is dat een foto van je familie? | Is that a photo of your family? |
| Kofi | Ja. Dat zijn mijn ouders, in Accra. | Yes. Those are my parents, in Accra. |
| Sanne | Hoe oud zijn ze? | How old are they? |
| Kofi | Mijn vader is achtenzestig en mijn moeder is vijfenzestig. | My father is sixty-eight and my mother is sixty-five. |
| Sanne | En wie is die vrouw? | And who is that woman? |
| Kofi | Dat is mijn zus, Ama. Ze is lerares. En dit is mijn broer Kwame. Hij woont in Londen. | That's my sister, Ama. She's a teacher. And this is my brother Kwame. He lives in London. |
| Sanne | Heeft Ama kinderen? | Does Ama have children? |
| Kofi | Ja, ze heeft drie kinderen: twee zonen en een dochter. | Yes, she has three children: two sons and a daughter. |
| Sanne | Wat een grote familie! | What a big family! |
| Kofi | Ja, in Ghana is dat normaal. En jij? Heb jij broers of zussen? | Yes, in Ghana that's normal. And you? Do you have brothers or sisters? |
| Sanne | Ik heb één broer. Hij heet Joost en hij woont in Gent. | I have one brother. His name is Joost and he lives in Ghent. |

### Dialogue 2: Op de verjaardag (At the birthday party)

Mei goes to the birthday of Ingrid, the mother of her boyfriend Daan. The family sits in a circle in the living room. Mei shakes hands with everyone.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daan | Mam, dit is Mei. | Mum, this is Mei. |
| Mei | Goedemiddag! Gefeliciteerd met uw verjaardag! Deze bloemen zijn voor u. | Good afternoon! Happy birthday! These flowers are for you. |
| Ingrid | Dank je wel, wat mooi! En zeg maar je, hoor. | Thank you, how beautiful! And just call me je. |
| Mei | (to a man in the circle) Gefeliciteerd met uw zus! Ik ben Mei. | Congratulations on your sister! I'm Mei. |
| Kees | Dank je. Kees. Ik ben de oom van Daan. | Thanks. Kees. I'm Daan's uncle. |
| Mei | (to an old man) Gefeliciteerd met uw dochter! | Congratulations on your daughter! |
| Opa | Dank je, meid. Ben jij de vriendin van Daan? | Thanks, girl. Are you Daan's girlfriend? |
| Mei | Ja, dat klopt. | Yes, that's right. |
| Opa | Welkom in de familie! Dat zijn mijn kleinkinderen, daar bij het raam. | Welcome to the family! Those are my grandchildren, over there by the window. |
| Ingrid | Mei, wil je koffie en een stukje taart? | Mei, would you like coffee and a piece of cake? |
| Mei | Ja, graag! | Yes, please! |

## Part 3: Grammar

**Remember:** **de**, **het** and **een** were taught in A0.3, and spelling rule 2 in A0.4. 3A and 3B are a short refresher with one or two extra rules. **New in this unit:** plurals (3C), whose (3D) and pointing with deze and die (3E).

### 3A. de and het (the)

Dutch has two words for "the". Every singular noun is either a **de-word** or a **het-word**. About two out of three nouns are de-words.

| | de | het |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which nouns | most nouns, including most people | about one third of nouns |
| Examples | de vader, de zus, de man, de vrouw | het kind, het gezin, het meisje, het huis |
| Plural | **always de**: de kinderen, de huizen | never het in the plural |

**Rules that always work:**

1. **All plurals take de:** het kind → de kinderen.
2. **Small words ending in -je are het** (diminutives): het meisje, het broertje (little brother), het kopje.
3. **Languages are het:** het Nederlands, het Engels.
4. **Most words for people are de**, even when the person is female: de zus, de moeder. The big exceptions are **het kind** and **het meisje**.

For everything else, learn the article with the word. You will hear the right one thousands of times, and the app keeps testing it.

### 3B. een (a, an)

**Een** works for both de-words and het-words. It is pronounced "un" (with a schwa), not like **één** (one), which has accents and a long "ayn" sound.

| | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific | **de** zoon, **het** kind | **de** zonen, **de** kinderen |
| Not specific | **een** zoon, **een** kind | zonen, kinderen (no word) |

- Ik heb **een** zus. (I have a sister.)
- Ik heb **één** zus, niet twee. (I have **one** sister, not two.)
- Ik heb broers. (I have brothers. There's no "een" in the plural.)

### 3C. Making plurals

**Rule 1: Most nouns add -en.**

| Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- |
| de vrouw | de vrouwen |
| de vriend | de vrienden |
| de nicht | de nichten |
| het boek | de boeken |

**Rule 2: Add -s to nouns ending in an unstressed -el, -em, -en, -er, -je, and to most nouns ending in -e.**

| Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- |
| de vader | de vaders |
| de dochter | de dochters |
| de broer | de broers |
| de tante | de tantes |
| het meisje | de meisjes |
| de tafel | de tafels |

**Rule 3: Add 's to nouns ending in -a, -o, -u, -i or -y.** The apostrophe keeps the vowel long.

| Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- |
| de opa | de opa's |
| de oma | de oma's |
| de foto | de foto's |
| de baby | de baby's |
| de collega | de collega's |

**Rule 4: When you add -en, use the spelling rules from A0.4.**

| What happens | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short vowel: double the consonant | de zus, de man, de kat | de zussen, de mannen, de katten |
| Long vowel: drop one vowel letter | de zoon, de buur, het jaar | de zonen, de buren, de jaren |
| f becomes v | de neef, de brief | de neven, de brieven |
| s becomes z | het huis, de reis | de huizen, de reizen |

The sound doesn't change in rows two to four. Only the spelling changes, so the vowel stays long or short as it was.

**Rule 5: Learn the irregular ones.**

| Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- |
| het kind | de kinderen |
| het kleinkind | de kleinkinderen |
| de stad | de steden |
| de dag | de dagen |
| het familielid | de familieleden |

### 3D. Whose? mijn, je, zijn, haar...

| Pronoun | Possessive | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ik | **mijn** (my) | mijn vader |
| jij / je | **jouw** (stressed) or **je** (your) | je moeder; Is dat **jouw** zus? |
| u | **uw** (your, formal) | uw dochter |
| hij | **zijn** (his) | zijn vrouw |
| zij / ze | **haar** (her) | haar man |
| wij / we | **ons** with het-words, **onze** with de-words and plurals | ons kind, onze zoon, onze kinderen |
| jullie | **jullie** (your, plural) | jullie ouders |
| zij / ze | **hun** (their) | hun oma |

**Watch out:**

- **zijn** means both "to be" and "his": Zijn broer is lang. (His brother is tall.)
- **haar** means both "her" and "hair": Haar haar is blond. (Her hair is blond.)
- **ons/onze** is the only one that changes with de and het. That's another reason to learn the article.

### 3E. Pointing: dit, dat, deze, die

| | Near (this, these) | Far (that, those) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| het-word | **dit** kind | **dat** kind |
| de-word | **deze** man | **die** man |
| plural | **deze** kinderen | **die** kinderen |

To introduce someone, use **dit** or **dat** for every word, with **is** for one person and **zijn** for more:

- **Dit is** mijn broer. (This is my brother.)
- **Dat zijn** mijn ouders. (Those are my parents.)

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |

| de meisje | het meisje | -je words are het |

| ons zoon | onze zoon | zoon is a de-word |

## Part 4: Pronunciation

### 4A. The schwa

The **schwa** is the weak, short "uh" sound at the end of English "sofa". It's the most common vowel in Dutch. You hear it in every unstressed **e** in these places:

| Where | Word | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The words de, het, een, je, ze, we | de vader | "duh VAA-dur" |
| Ending -e | tante | "TAN-tuh" |
| Ending -en | vrouwen | "VROW-uh" |
| Unstressed -el, -er | tafel, vader | "TAA-ful", "VAA-dur" |
| Prefixes be-, ge-, ver- | getrouwd, verlegen | "khuh-TROWT", "vur-LAY-khuh" |
| Ending -ig | aardig, gezellig | "AAR-duch", "khuh-ZEL-uch" |
| Ending -lijk | moeilijk (difficult) | "MOOY-luk" |

**Two things English speakers get wrong:**

1. **The -ig ending isn't "ig".** It's a schwa plus the Dutch g: aardig sounds like "AAR-dukh".
2. **The -lijk ending isn't "like".** It's just "luk". The spelling ij doesn't count here.

### 4B. The silent n in -en

In everyday Netherlands Dutch, the **n** at the end of **-en** is usually silent. Newsreaders and careful speakers say it, most other people drop it. Always **write** -en.

| Word | Careful speech | Everyday speech |
| --- | --- | --- |
| kinderen | KIN-duh-run | KIN-duh-ruh |
| vrouwen | VROW-un | VROW-uh |
| wonen | WOH-nun | WOH-nuh |
| zonen | ZOH-nun | ZOH-nuh |

Use the everyday form when you speak. It sounds natural and is correct standard Dutch. In Flanders, and in the north-east of the Netherlands, you'll hear the n more often.

## Part 8: Culture & review

### Family life the Dutch way

- **De kring (the circle):** at a Dutch birthday, guests sit in a circle in the living room. When you arrive, you shake hands with **everyone** and congratulate them, not just the person whose birthday it is. "Gefeliciteerd met je zus!" sounds strange in English, but it's what everyone says. Close friends and family give three kisses on the cheeks.
- **Koffie met taart:** you get coffee with one piece of cake. Snacks and drinks come later.
- **The birthday calendar:** many Dutch homes have a **verjaardagskalender** on the inside of the toilet door, listing everyone's birthday. Really!
- **A new baby:** parents send a geboortekaartje. Visitors get **beschuit met muisjes**: a round rusk with butter and pink or blue aniseed sprinkles, pink for a girl and blue for a boy. Always ask before you visit.
- **Gezin or familie?** Your **gezin** is the household (parents and children). Your **familie** is everyone you're related to. "Mijn familie woont in Ghana" means your relatives in general.
- **Flemish note:** in Belgium you'll often hear **mama** and **papa**, and in some regions **bomma** and **bompa** for grandma and grandpa. In the Netherlands, **mam**, **pap**, **oma** and **opa** are more usual.
