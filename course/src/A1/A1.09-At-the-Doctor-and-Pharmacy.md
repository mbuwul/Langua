# A1.9 At the Doctor and Pharmacy (Bij de huisarts en de apotheek)

## Unit at a glance

After this unit (about 9 hours) you can phone the GP's practice, explain what's wrong, understand the doctor's instructions, collect medicine at the pharmacy and call in sick for yourself or your child. You'll learn the **imperative** (the form for instructions: Ga zitten. Neem twee tabletten.) and how to say whose something is with **van** and **die van mij**. In pronunciation you practise speaking clearly when it matters most: on the phone and under stress.

**You will be able to:**

- Name about 20 parts of the body and 15 common complaints
- Say what's wrong and since when: Ik heb sinds gisteren koorts.
- Understand and give instructions with the imperative, politely
- Understand the dose on a medicine label: twee keer per dag één tablet
- Ask and say who something belongs to: Van wie is deze tas? Die is van mij.
- Call in sick and give key information clearly on the phone

**Builds on:** A1.2 (possessives), A1.6 (modal verbs), A1.7 (separable verbs, times and dates).

## Part 1: Situation

### Dialogue 1: De huisarts bellen (Phoning the GP)

Kofi has been ill for three days. He phones his GP's practice. The doctor's assistant answers and decides how urgent it is.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assistente | Huisartsenpraktijk Lindeplein, met Ilse. | Lindeplein GP practice, Ilse speaking. |
| Kofi | Goedemorgen, met Kofi Mensah. Ik ben ziek en ik wil graag een afspraak maken. | Good morning, this is Kofi Mensah. I'm ill and I'd like to make an appointment. |
| Assistente | Wat is uw geboortedatum? | What's your date of birth? |
| Kofi | Vijftien april negentienhonderdnegentig. | Fifteen April nineteen ninety. |
| Assistente | Dank u. Wat zijn uw klachten? | Thank you. What are your symptoms? |
| Kofi | Ik heb sinds drie dagen koorts en veel keelpijn. Ik kan bijna niet slikken. | I've had a fever for three days and a very sore throat. I can hardly swallow. |
| Assistente | Hoe hoog is de koorts? | How high is the fever? |
| Kofi | Vanochtend was het 39 graden. | This morning it was 39 degrees. |
| Assistente | Dan wil de dokter u vandaag zien. Kunt u om tien over twee komen? | Then the doctor wants to see you today. Can you come at ten past two? |
| Kofi | Ja, dat kan. | Yes, I can. |
| Assistente | Neem uw medicijnen mee als u die gebruikt. Tot vanmiddag. | Bring your medicines if you take any. See you this afternoon. |

### Dialogue 2: Bij de apotheek (At the pharmacy)

The doctor has prescribed antibiotics. Kofi collects them at the pharmacy.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apotheker | Goedemiddag. Zegt u het maar. | Good afternoon. How can I help? |
| Kofi | Ik kom een recept ophalen. De huisarts heeft het net gestuurd. | I've come to collect a prescription. The GP has just sent it. |
| Apotheker | Wat is uw naam en geboortedatum? | What's your name and date of birth? |
| Kofi | Kofi Mensah, 15 april 1990. | Kofi Mensah, 15 April 1990. |
| Apotheker | Ja, hier is het. Dit is een antibioticum. Neem drie keer per dag één tablet, met water. | Yes, here it is. This is an antibiotic. Take one tablet three times a day, with water. |
| Kofi | Voor of na het eten? | Before or after food? |
| Apotheker | Dat maakt niet uit. Maar maak de kuur altijd af, ook als u zich beter voelt. | It doesn't matter. But always finish the course, even if you feel better. |
| Kofi | Hoe lang moet ik ze nemen? | How long do I have to take them? |
| Apotheker | Zeven dagen. Drink geen alcohol en lees de bijsluiter goed. | Seven days. Don't drink alcohol and read the leaflet carefully. |
| Kofi | Dank u. O, is deze paraplu van u? | Thank you. Oh, is this umbrella yours? |
| Apotheker | Nee, die is niet van mij. Hij is van de vorige klant, denk ik. | No, it's not mine. It belongs to the previous customer, I think. |

**Notice:** "was", "heeft gestuurd" and "denk ik" are past tense and fixed phrases. Learn them as chunks for now; A2.1 explains the past.

**Check questions:**

1. How long has Kofi been ill? (a) one day (b) three days (c) a week. **Answer: b**
2. What are his symptoms? (a) headache and cough (b) fever and a sore throat (c) stomach ache. **Answer: b**
3. What time is his appointment? (a) 14:10 (b) 13:50 (c) 10:00. **Answer: a**
4. How often does Kofi take the antibiotic? (a) once a day (b) twice a day (c) three times a day. **Answer: c**
5. What must he not do? (a) drink water (b) drink alcohol (c) eat. **Answer: b**
6. Whose umbrella is it? (a) Kofi's (b) the pharmacist's (c) the previous customer's. **Answer: c**

## Part 3: Grammar

### 3A. The imperative

The imperative is the form for instructions, advice and requests. It's very easy: **use the stem** (the ik form).

| Infinitive | Imperative | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| komen | kom | **Kom** binnen! |
| nemen | neem | **Neem** twee tabletten. |
| drinken | drink | **Drink** veel water. |
| gaan | ga | **Ga** zitten. |
| blijven | blijf | **Blijf** thuis. |
| lezen | lees | **Lees** de bijsluiter. |
| zijn | wees | **Wees** voorzichtig! (Be careful!) |

**Rules:**

1. The imperative goes **first** in the sentence. There's no subject.
2. **Separable verbs** split, with the prefix at the end: **Doe** uw jas **uit**. **Maak** de kuur **af**. **Bel** morgen **terug**.
3. **Negative:** niet or geen after the verb or near the end: **Rook niet.** **Drink geen** alcohol.

### 3B. Making it polite

A bare imperative can sound bossy. Dutch speakers soften it with small words:

| Softener | Effect | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| maar | friendly, inviting | Gaat u **maar** zitten. Kom **maar** binnen. |
| even | "just quickly" | Doe **even** de deur dicht. |
| eens | casual | Zeg **eens** "aaa". |
| alstublieft / alsjeblieft | please | Neem plaats, **alstublieft**. |
| polite u-form | formal | **Komt u** binnen. **Gaat u** maar zitten. |

The polite **u**-form is a question-shaped imperative: **Gaat u maar zitten** = "Please sit down". It uses the u verb form (gaat, komt) followed by **u**. Doctors, assistants and shop staff use it all the time.

**Tip:** with friends and colleagues, a request with **kun je** (A1.6) is often softer than an imperative: **Kun je even** de deur dichtdoen?

### 3C. Whose is it? van and die van mij

**van + person** = 's

- de tas **van** Ahmed (Ahmed's bag)
- het recept **van** mijn moeder (my mother's prescription)
- de dokter **van** de kinderen (the children's doctor)

In speech you'll also hear names with **'s** or **-s**: **Ahmeds** tas, **Ilse's** auto. With **van** you can never go wrong.

**Van wie?** (Whose?)

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Van wie** is deze paraplu? | Hij is **van mij**. |
| **Van wie** is dit recept? | Het is **van mijn vader**. |
| Is die jas **van jou**? | Nee, **die van mij** is blauw. |

**Mine, yours, his:** use **van** + the object pronoun:

| English | Dutch |
| --- | --- |
| mine | van mij |
| yours (je) | van jou |
| yours (u) | van u |
| his | van hem |
| hers | van haar |
| ours | van ons |
| yours (jullie) | van jullie |
| theirs | van hen (in speech: van ze) |

**Instead of repeating the noun:** **die van mij** (mine, de-words and plurals), **dat van mij** (mine, het-words).

- Jouw jas is groen, **die van mij** is blauw.
- Zijn recept is klaar, **dat van mij** niet.

## Part 4: Pronunciation

### 4A. Clear speech on the phone

On the phone, there are no gestures, the sound is worse, and the other person often types what you say. When you're ill or stressed, you speak faster and less clearly. Train these four habits until they're automatic:

| Habit | How | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slow down for key information | Pause before and after names, dates and numbers | "Mijn naam is ... Kofi Mensah." |
| Numbers in small groups | Phone numbers in pairs or single digits | 06 – 12 – 34 – 56 – 78 |
| Stress the important word | Put the sentence stress on the new information | Ik heb **koorts**. Sinds **drie** dagen. |
| Repeat and check | Say important things back | "Tien over twee. Klopt dat?" |

### 4B. Dates of birth and numbers

Dutch speakers say years as two halves: **1990** = negentienhonderdnegentig (literally nineteen-hundred-ninety). From 2000: **2008** = tweeduizend acht, **2015** = tweeduizend vijftien.

Phone numbers: **06** is said "nul zes". Mobile numbers have ten digits: 06 12 34 56 78. Say them in pairs (twaalf, vierendertig...) or one by one (een, twee, drie, vier...).

## Part 8: Culture & review

### Health care the Dutch way

- **The GP is the gatekeeper:** you can't go to a hospital specialist without a referral (**verwijzing**) from your GP, except in an emergency. Register with a GP near your home as soon as you move.
- **The assistant decides:** when you phone, the doctor's assistant asks questions and decides if you need to come in today, later, or not at all. Answer clearly and say if it's urgent.
- **"Take paracetamol":** Dutch GPs are careful with medicines and antibiotics. For a cold or flu you'll often hear: rest, drink water, and take paracetamol. Many newcomers find this surprising.
- **Pharmacy or chemist:** prescription medicines come from the **apotheek**. Paracetamol, cold remedies and plasters are at the **drogist** (like Kruidvat or Etos) and in supermarkets.
- **Evenings and weekends:** call the **huisartsenpost**. For life-threatening emergencies, call **112**. For non-urgent police matters: **0900-8844**.
- **Insurance:** health insurance (**zorgverzekering**) is compulsory for everyone who lives or works in the Netherlands. You pay the first part of some costs yourself each year (**het eigen risico**), but GP visits are free.
- **Calling in sick:** you call your employer before work starts, usually by phone. Your employer may not ask what's wrong with you; only the company doctor (**bedrijfsarts**) may ask.
- **Flemish note:** in Belgium you usually pay the doctor directly and get most of it back from your health fund (**het ziekenfonds** or **de mutualiteit**). You can see a specialist without a referral. People often say **de dokter** for the GP, and **een voorschrift** for a prescription.
