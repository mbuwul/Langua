# A1.6 Getting Around (Op weg: trein, fiets en OV)

## Unit at a glance

After this unit (about 9 hours) you can travel by train, bus, tram and bike, buy a ticket, ask about times and platforms, and understand the main rules on the road. You'll learn the four **modal verbs** that make Dutch sound natural straight away: **kunnen** (can), **willen** (want), **moeten** (must, have to) and **mogen** (may, be allowed to). In pronunciation you learn how Dutch speakers join words together.

**You will be able to:**

- Ask for information at a station and understand the answer
- Use kunnen, willen, moeten and mogen with an infinitive at the end of the sentence
- Ask permission politely: Mag ik ...? Kan ik ...?
- Say what is allowed and what isn't: Dat mag niet. Je moet hier afstappen.
- Understand a simple station announcement, including delays and platform changes
- Link words together the way Dutch speakers do: Kan ik → "ka-nik"

**Builds on:** A0.6 (times), A1.3 (present tense, verb second), A1.4 (questions, niet/geen), A1.5 (directions, prepositions).

## Part 1: Situation

### Dialogue 1: Bij de informatiebalie (At the information desk)

Kofi is at Utrecht Centraal station. He wants to go to Amsterdam with his bike.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kofi | Goedemorgen. Ik wil naar Amsterdam Centraal. Moet ik overstappen? | Good morning. I want to go to Amsterdam Central. Do I have to change trains? |
| Medewerker | Nee, de intercity gaat direct. Hij vertrekt om 10.14 van spoor 5. | No, the intercity goes direct. It leaves at 10:14 from platform 5. |
| Kofi | Kan ik met mijn bankpas betalen? | Can I pay with my bank card? |
| Medewerker | Ja, u kunt met uw bankpas inchecken bij het poortje. U moet ook weer uitchecken in Amsterdam. | Yes, you can check in with your bank card at the gate. You also have to check out again in Amsterdam. |
| Kofi | Mag ik mijn fiets meenemen in de trein? | May I take my bike on the train? |
| Medewerker | Ja, maar niet in de spits. En u moet een dagkaart fiets kopen. | Yes, but not in the rush hour. And you have to buy a bike day ticket. |
| Kofi | Wanneer is de spits? | When is the rush hour? |
| Medewerker | Van half zeven tot negen uur 's ochtends en van half vijf tot zes uur 's middags. | From half past six till nine in the morning and from half past four till six in the afternoon. |
| Kofi | Dan kan ik nu gaan. Dank u wel! | Then I can go now. Thank you! |

### Dialogue 2: Fietsles (A cycling lesson)

Mei can ride a bike, but not in Dutch traffic. Daan helps her. They use **je**.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mei | Ik kan wel fietsen, maar ik vind het verkeer eng. | I can ride a bike, but I find the traffic scary. |
| Daan | Dat snap ik. Eerst de regels. Je moet altijd op het fietspad fietsen. | I understand. First the rules. You always have to cycle on the cycle path. |
| Mei | Mag ik op de stoep fietsen? | Am I allowed to cycle on the pavement? |
| Daan | Nee, dat mag niet. De stoep is voor mensen die lopen. | No, that's not allowed. The pavement is for people walking. |
| Mei | En mag ik bellen op de fiets? | And can I phone on the bike? |
| Daan | Nee! Je mag je telefoon niet vasthouden. Dan krijg je een boete. | No! You can't hold your phone. Then you get a fine. |
| Mei | Oké. Wil je samen naar de markt fietsen? | OK. Do you want to cycle to the market together? |
| Daan | Goed idee. Kun je je licht aandoen? Het wordt donker. | Good idea. Can you turn your light on? It's getting dark. |
| Mei | Moet dat? | Do I have to? |
| Daan | Ja, in het donker moet je licht hebben. | Yes, in the dark you must have a light. |

**Notice:** "inchecken", "meenemen", "vasthouden" and "aandoen" are separable verbs, but here they stay in one piece because they're at the **end** of the sentence after a modal verb. That makes modal verbs a great way to use these verbs before A1.7.

**Check questions:**

1. Does Kofi have to change trains? (a) yes, in Amsterdam (b) no, the train is direct (c) yes, twice. **Answer: b**
2. How can Kofi pay? (a) only with cash (b) with his bank card at the gate (c) on the train. **Answer: b**
3. When can't he take his bike? (a) at weekends (b) in the rush hour (c) after 6 pm. **Answer: b**
4. Where must Mei cycle? (a) on the pavement (b) on the cycle path (c) on the road. **Answer: b**
5. Why must Mei turn on her light? (a) it's getting dark (b) it's raining (c) the police are there. **Answer: a**

## Part 3: Grammar

### 3A. The four modal verbs

| | kunnen (can) | willen (want) | moeten (must, have to) | mogen (may, be allowed to) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ik | kan | wil | moet | mag |
| jij / je | kunt (or kan) | wilt (or wil) | moet | mag |
| u | kunt | wilt | moet | mag |
| hij / zij / het | kan | wil | moet | mag |
| wij / jullie / zij | kunnen | willen | moeten | mogen |

These four verbs are **irregular**: ik, hij and zij have the same form, with **no -t**. For jij and u, kunt and wilt are standard. You'll hear **je kan** and **je wil** in everyday speech too; both are correct.

### 3B. The infinitive goes to the end

The modal verb takes the normal verb position (second). The main verb goes to the **very end** of the sentence, in its infinitive form.

| 1 | Modal (2) | Middle | Infinitive (end) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ik | wil | een kaartje | kopen. |
| Je | moet | op het fietspad | fietsen. |
| Morgen | kan | ik niet | komen. |
| U | mag | hier niet | parkeren. |
| Wij | moeten | om acht uur bij het station | zijn. |

**Questions** start with the modal: **Kan** ik met mijn bankpas **betalen**? **Mag** ik hier **zitten**?

**Negatives:** **niet** goes just before the infinitive (or before a place word): Ik kan **niet** komen. Je mag hier **niet** fietsen. Use **geen** with een-words and plurals: Je mag **geen** fiets meenemen.

**English speakers' most common mistake:** putting the infinitive straight after the modal, as in English. "Ik wil kopen een kaartje" is wrong. Think of the sentence as a sandwich: the modal and the infinitive are the two slices of bread, and everything else goes in between.

### 3C. Modals without a second verb

When the meaning is clear, Dutch often leaves out the infinitive, especially **gaan** and **doen**:

- Ik **moet** naar Amsterdam. (I have to go to Amsterdam.)
- Ik **wil** naar huis. (I want to go home.)
- Dat **mag** niet. (That's not allowed.)
- Dat **kan**! (That's possible! / Sure!)
- **Moet** dat? (Do I have to?)

### 3D. moeten, hoeven and mogen in the negative

English "must not" and "don't have to" are different, and so are the Dutch forms.

| Meaning | Dutch | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It's forbidden | mag niet | Je **mag** hier **niet** roken. (You must not smoke here.) |
| It isn't necessary | hoeft niet te | Je **hoeft niet te** betalen. (You don't have to pay.) |

**Watch out:** "Je moet niet roken" sounds like advice ("you shouldn't smoke"), not a rule. For rules, use **mag niet**. Learn **hoeft niet te + infinitive** as a pattern; it's the only modal-type verb that needs **te**.

### 3E. Polite requests

| Polite | Very polite | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kan ik ...? | Zou ik ... kunnen? | Can I ...? / Could I ...? |
| Kunt u ...? | Zou u ... kunnen? | Can you ...? / Could you ...? |
| Mag ik ...? | | May I ...? |
| Ik wil ... | Ik wil graag ... | I'd like ... |

**Tip:** "Ik wil een kaartje" can sound a bit blunt. **Ik wil graag een kaartje** or **Mag ik een kaartje?** is friendlier. You'll learn more about graag in A1.10.

### Typical mistakes for English speakers

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ik wil kopen een kaartje. | Ik wil een kaartje kopen. | Infinitive at the end |
| Hij kant komen. | Hij kan komen. | No -t on modals for hij/zij |
| Ik moet te werken. | Ik moet werken. | No te after the four modals |
| Kan ik betaal met pin? | Kan ik met pin betalen? | Infinitive form, at the end |
| Je moet niet roken hier. | Je mag hier niet roken. | Rule = mag niet |
| Je moet niet betalen. | Je hoeft niet te betalen. | Not necessary = hoeft niet te |

## Part 4: Pronunciation

### 4A. Linking words together

In normal speech, Dutch speakers don't pause between words. A word that ends in a consonant joins onto a next word that starts with a vowel, so the two sound like one.

| Written | Sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| Kan ik ...? | "ka-nik" |
| Mag ik ...? | "ma-chik" |
| Is er ...? | "i-ser" |
| Wat is dat? | "wa-tis dat" |
| Heb ik ...? | "he-pik" |
| om acht uur | "o-macht-uur" |

**Notice** in "heb ik": the b sounds like **p**, because b at the end of a word is always devoiced (A0.4), and it stays devoiced when it links on.

### 4B. Short forms you'll hear

| Full form | Everyday form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| het | 't | 't Is laat. |
| ik | 'k | 'k Weet het niet. |
| hij | -ie (after a verb) | Komt-ie? (Is he coming?) |
| een | 'n | Ik heb 'n fiets. |

You don't have to use these, but you must recognise them. Write the full forms.

### 4C. Linking drill (8 phrases, scored)

Say each phrase fast and smoothly, as one unit. The app checks that there's no gap between the linked words.

1. Kan ik hier pinnen?
2. Mag ik even langs?
3. Is er een bus naar het centrum?
4. Wat is het spoor?
5. Heb ik een kaartje nodig?
6. Om acht uur moet ik op het werk zijn.
7. Kun je even helpen?
8. Wil je een kopje koffie?

**Final task:** record your role-play questions from Part 6B. It's scored on linking and on the infinitive at the end.

>
> U mag uw fiets meenemen in de trein, maar niet altijd.

## Part 8: Culture & review

### Travelling the Dutch way

- **Check in, check out:** on all trains, buses, trams and metros you hold your bank card, phone or OV-chipkaart against the reader when you get on **and** when you get off. Forget to check out, and you pay a high fixed fare.
- **The bike is king:** cycle paths are red or have a bike symbol. Cyclists have their own traffic lights. A bell, working brakes and lights in the dark are compulsory. Holding a phone while cycling has been illegal since 2019.
- **The OV-fiets:** at most stations you can rent a blue-and-yellow bike for a day with a subscription.
- **Punctuality:** Dutch trains run every 10 to 30 minutes on most routes. People plan with the **NS app** or **9292**, and being five minutes late to a meeting needs an apology.
- **Quiet zone:** in a carriage marked **stilte** (silence), you mustn't phone or talk loudly.
- **Flemish note:** in Belgium, trains are run by the **NMBS** and buses and trams by **De Lijn** (in Flanders). People say **het perron** and **de tram** too, and you may hear **een abonnement** for a season ticket in both countries.
