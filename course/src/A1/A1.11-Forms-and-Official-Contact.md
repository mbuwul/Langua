# A1.11 Forms & Official Contact (Formulieren en instanties)

## Unit at a glance

After this unit (about 9 hours) you can deal with Dutch officials: fill in a form, register a change at the town hall, give your details on the phone, spell your name and email address, and ask someone to repeat or explain. You'll learn exactly when to use **u** and when **je**, and how formal letters and emails start and end. In pronunciation you learn to spell aloud fluently, the skill every newcomer needs on the phone.

**You will be able to:**

- Understand and fill in the fields on a Dutch form (voornaam, tussenvoegsel, burgerlijke staat...)
- Spell names, email addresses and postcodes aloud, quickly and clearly
- Choose u or je correctly, and switch when someone says "Zeg maar je"
- Ask for repetition and explanation politely: Kunt u dat herhalen? Wat bedoelt u?
- Use the right greeting and closing in formal and informal emails
- Navigate a phone menu and a visit to the town hall

**Builds on:** A0.1 (alphabet), A0.3 (numbers), A1.1 (personal details), A1.7 (dates), A1.9 (clear speech, imperative).

## Part 1: Situation

### Dialogue 1: Aan de balie (At the counter)

Kofi has moved to a new address in the same town. He goes to the town hall to register his move. He has taken a number and waited.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medewerker | Nummer 47? Goedemorgen. Waarmee kan ik u helpen? | Number 47? Good morning. How can I help you? |
| Kofi | Goedemorgen. Ik ben verhuisd en ik wil mijn nieuwe adres doorgeven. | Good morning. I've moved and I want to report my new address. |
| Medewerker | Prima. Mag ik uw identiteitsbewijs? | Fine. May I have your ID? |
| Kofi | Alstublieft, mijn verblijfsvergunning. | Here you are, my residence permit. |
| Medewerker | Dank u. Wat is uw nieuwe adres? | Thank you. What's your new address? |
| Kofi | Lindelaan 8, eerste verdieping. | Lindelaan 8, first floor. |
| Medewerker | Kunt u de straatnaam spellen? | Can you spell the street name? |
| Kofi | L-I-N-D-E-L-A-A-N. Lindelaan. | L-I-N-D-E-L-A-A-N. Lindelaan. |
| Medewerker | En het huisnummer is 8. Is dat 8 of 8 hoog? | And the house number is 8. Is that 8 or 8 upstairs? |
| Kofi | Sorry, wat bedoelt u met "hoog"? | Sorry, what do you mean by "hoog"? |
| Medewerker | Woont u op de begane grond of boven? | Do you live on the ground floor or upstairs? |
| Kofi | Boven, op de eerste verdieping. | Upstairs, on the first floor. |
| Medewerker | Dan is het 8-I. Wat is de postcode? | Then it's 8-I. What's the postcode? |
| Kofi | 3521 AB. | 3521 AB. |
| Medewerker | Prima. U krijgt binnen vijf dagen een bevestiging per post. Woont er nog iemand op dit adres? | Fine. You'll get a confirmation by post within five days. Does anyone else live at this address? |
| Kofi | Nee, ik woon alleen. | No, I live alone. |

**Notice:** "Ik ben verhuisd" is past tense; learn it as a phrase. **8-I** is said "acht één hoog" or "acht eerste": it marks the first floor.

### Dialogue 2: Aan de telefoon (On the phone)

Grace phones her energy company to change her email address.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medewerker | Goedemiddag, u spreekt met Tim van Groen Energie. Wat kan ik voor u doen? | Good afternoon, this is Tim from Groen Energie. What can I do for you? |
| Grace | Goedemiddag, met Grace Mbah. Ik wil mijn e-mailadres veranderen. | Good afternoon, this is Grace Mbah. I'd like to change my email address. |
| Medewerker | Dat kan. Wat is uw klantnummer? | That's possible. What's your customer number? |
| Grace | Twee, vier, zeven, acht, nul, drie, één. | Two, four, seven, eight, zero, three, one. |
| Medewerker | En uw postcode en huisnummer? | And your postcode and house number? |
| Grace | 3521 AB, nummer 12a. | 3521 AB, number 12a. |
| Medewerker | Dank u. Wat is het nieuwe e-mailadres? | Thank you. What's the new email address? |
| Grace | g punt mbah, apenstaartje, mail punt com. | g dot mbah, at, mail dot com. |
| Medewerker | Sorry, kunt u dat langzaam spellen? | Sorry, could you spell that slowly? |
| Grace | Natuurlijk. G, punt, M van Maria, B van Bernard, A, H. Apenstaartje, mail, punt, com. | Of course. G, dot, M for Maria, B for Bernard, A, H. At, mail, dot, com. |
| Medewerker | Dank u. Ik herhaal: g.mbah@mail.com. Klopt dat? | Thank you. I'll repeat: g.mbah@mail.com. Is that right? |
| Grace | Ja, dat klopt. | Yes, that's right. |
| Medewerker | U krijgt een mail om het te bevestigen. Kan ik u verder nog ergens mee helpen? | You'll get an email to confirm it. Can I help you with anything else? |
| Grace | Nee, dat was het. Dank u wel. | No, that's all. Thank you very much. |

**Check questions:**

1. Why does Kofi go to the town hall? (a) to get a passport (b) to report his new address (c) to get married. **Answer: b**
2. What does Kofi show as ID? (a) his passport (b) his residence permit (c) his driving licence. **Answer: b**
3. What does "8-I" mean? (a) number 8, first floor (b) number 81 (c) number 8, ground floor. **Answer: a**
4. When will Kofi get a confirmation? (a) today (b) within five days (c) within a month. **Answer: b**
5. What does Grace want to change? (a) her address (b) her email address (c) her bank account. **Answer: b**
6. How does the employee check the email address? (a) he spells it back (b) he sends a letter (c) he asks for her ID. **Answer: a**

## Part 3: Grammar

### 3A. u or je?

You've used both since A0.2. Here is the complete guide.

| Use **u** with | Use **je/jij** with |
| --- | --- |
| Officials, police, doctors, counter staff | Family and friends |
| People clearly older than you | Children and teenagers |
| Strangers in formal situations | Colleagues (in most companies) |
| Customers (if you work in a shop or office) | People your age in relaxed places |
| Anyone who uses u with you, until they switch | Anyone who says "Zeg maar je" |

**When in doubt, start with u.** Dutch people will quickly tell you if they prefer je: "Zeg maar je, hoor!" Then switch immediately. Using u after that sounds distant.

**You can also ask:** "Mag ik je zeggen?" or "Mag ik jij zeggen?" (May I call you je?)

**The u forms you need:**

| Verb | u form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| zijn | u bent | Bent u mevrouw Visser? |
| hebben | u heeft (or u hebt) | Heeft u een afspraak? |
| kunnen | u kunt | Kunt u dat spellen? |
| willen | u wilt | Wilt u even wachten? |
| regular verbs | u + stem + t | Woont u hier? Spreekt u Engels? |
| possessive | uw | Wat is uw adres? |
| imperative | stem + t + u (+ maar) | Komt u maar binnen. |

**Notice:** with **u**, the verb always keeps its -t, even in questions: **Woont u** hier? (but: **Woon je** hier?)

### 3B. Asking for help in a conversation

These phrases save you in every official situation, and in the exam.

| Situation | Formal (u) | Informal (je) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You didn't hear | Kunt u dat herhalen? | Kun je dat herhalen? |
| Too fast | Kunt u wat langzamer praten? | Kun je wat langzamer praten? |
| You don't know a word | Wat betekent "..."? | Wat betekent "..."? |
| You don't understand what they mean | Wat bedoelt u? | Wat bedoel je? |
| You need spelling | Kunt u dat spellen? | Kun je dat spellen? |
| Checking | Bedoelt u ...? Klopt dat? | Bedoel je ...? Klopt dat? |
| Giving yourself time | Een momentje, alstublieft. | Momentje. |
| You don't know | Dat weet ik niet. | Dat weet ik niet. |

### 3C. Formal and informal emails

| | Very formal | Formal (most common) | Informal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening, name unknown | Geachte heer, mevrouw, | Beste meneer, mevrouw, | (not used) |
| Opening, name known | Geachte heer De Vries, / Geachte mevrouw Visser, | Beste meneer De Vries, / Beste mevrouw Visser, | Hoi Sanne, / Hallo Kofi, |
| Closing | Hoogachtend, | Met vriendelijke groet, | Groetjes, / Groeten, |
| Pronoun | u | u | je |

**Rules:**

- After the opening, put a **comma**, then start the email on a new line **with a capital letter**.
- "Beste" + title is now standard for most emails to officials, schools and companies. "Geachte" is for letters to authorities, complaints and job applications (B1 and above).
- Never "Beste mevrouw Grace". Use the surname: **Beste mevrouw Mbah**.

### 3D. Names with van, de, van der

Many Dutch surnames start with a **tussenvoegsel** (prefix) like **van, de, van der, van den, ter**.

| With the first name or initials | Without the first name |
| --- | --- |
| Sanne **de** Boer | mevrouw **De** Boer |
| J. **van der** Berg | meneer **Van der** Berg |
| Dirk **van** Dam | Beste meneer **Van** Dam |

In the Netherlands, the prefix gets a capital letter only when there's no first name or initial in front. In alphabetical lists it's sorted under the main name: **De Boer** is under **B**. In Belgium, the prefix is written as registered, often with a capital: **Van Damme**, and sorted under **V**.

### Typical mistakes for English speakers

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Woon u hier? | Woont u hier? | u always keeps the -t |
| Kan u dat spellen? | Kunt u dat spellen? | kunnen + u = kunt |
| Wat is jouw adres? (to an official) | Wat is uw adres? | Officials: u, uw |
| Beste mevrouw Sanne, | Beste mevrouw De Boer, | Use the surname with mevrouw |
| Geachte mevrouw de Boer, | Geachte mevrouw De Boer, | Capital when no first name |
| Met vriendelijke groeten, Grace (to a friend) | Groetjes, Grace | Match the tone |
| Wat bedoelen? | Wat bedoelt u? | Full sentence with subject |

## Part 8: Culture & review

### Dealing with officials the Dutch way

- **The gemeente is your first stop:** you register your address there and get your **BSN** (citizen service number). You need your BSN for work, the bank, the doctor and taxes. Keep it private.
- **DigiD:** most government business is online. With **DigiD** (an app or username and password) you log in to the tax office, the gemeente, your health insurer and more.
- **Appointments:** for passports, ID cards and driving licences, you usually book online first. At the counter, take a **nummertje** and wait for your number on the screen.
- **Directness:** Dutch officials are friendly but direct and to the point. "Dat kan niet" (That's not possible) isn't rude, just a fact. Ask what you can do instead: "Wat kan ik dan doen?"
- **Formality is changing:** many companies and even some government bodies now use **je** in letters and websites. In person, start with **u** and follow the other person.
- **Flemish note:** in Belgium people use **u** more and for longer than in the Netherlands, including with colleagues. Your number is the **rijksregisternummer**, on your Belgian **eID** card. After you register a move at the **gemeentehuis** or **stadhuis**, a local police officer (**de wijkagent**) usually visits to check that you really live there.
