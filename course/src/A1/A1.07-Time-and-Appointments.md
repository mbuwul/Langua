# A1.7 Time & Appointments (Tijd en afspraken)

## Unit at a glance

After this unit (about 9 hours) you can make, change and cancel appointments, say dates and days, and talk about your week. You'll learn the days, months and ordinal numbers (first, second, third...), and the famous Dutch **separable verbs** (opstaan → ik sta op) that you've been learning as phrases since A1.3. In pronunciation you learn where the stress goes in these verbs.

**You will be able to:**

- Say the days of the week, the months and any date (op dinsdag 14 mei)
- Use ordinal numbers: eerste, tweede, derde, twintigste...
- Make, move and cancel an appointment by phone and in a message
- Use separable verbs in statements, questions and with modal verbs
- Use time words: morgen, overmorgen, volgende week, over twee weken
- Put the stress on the right part of separable and inseparable verbs

**Builds on:** A0.4 to A0.6 (numbers and clock times), A1.3 (daily routine, verb second), A1.6 (modal verbs).

## Part 1: Situation

### Dialogue 1: Een afspraak bij de kapper (An appointment at the hairdresser's)

Grace phones a hairdresser. She uses **u**.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kapper | Kapsalon Knip, goedemorgen, met Joyce. | Knip hair salon, good morning, Joyce speaking. |
| Grace | Goedemorgen, met Grace Mbah. Ik wil graag een afspraak maken. | Good morning, this is Grace Mbah. I'd like to make an appointment. |
| Kapper | Natuurlijk. Wanneer komt het u uit? | Of course. When suits you? |
| Grace | Kan het op donderdag? | Is Thursday possible? |
| Kapper | Donderdag de twaalfde... Om tien uur of om half drie. | Thursday the twelfth... At ten or at half past two. |
| Grace | Om tien uur moet ik werken. Half drie komt goed uit. | At ten I have to work. Half past two suits me. |
| Kapper | Prima. Donderdag twaalf juni om half drie. Op welke naam? | Fine. Thursday 12 June at half past two. In what name? |
| Grace | Mbah. M-B-A-H. | Mbah. M-B-A-H. |
| Kapper | Dank u. Als u niet kunt komen, belt u dan op tijd af? | Thank you. If you can't come, will you call to cancel in time? |
| Grace | Ja, natuurlijk. Tot donderdag! | Yes, of course. See you Thursday! |

**Notice:** "Belt u ... af" is the separable verb **afbellen** (to call to cancel). The verb part goes second and **af** goes to the end. That's exactly what this unit teaches.

### Dialogue 2: Een appje (A text message)

Sanne and Kofi have an appointment for their language practice. Sanne sends a message.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sanne | Hoi Kofi! Ik moet onze afspraak van woensdag helaas afzeggen. Mijn dochter is ziek. 😕 | Hi Kofi! Unfortunately I have to cancel our appointment on Wednesday. My daughter is ill. |
| Kofi | Geen probleem! Beterschap voor Fleur. Zullen we een nieuwe dag afspreken? | No problem! Get well soon to Fleur. Shall we arrange a new day? |
| Sanne | Graag. Kun jij volgende week maandag? | Yes, please. Can you do next Monday? |
| Kofi | Maandag werk ik tot zes uur. Dinsdagavond kan wel. | On Monday I work until six. Tuesday evening is possible. |
| Sanne | Dinsdag de achttiende om half acht? | Tuesday the eighteenth at half past seven? |
| Kofi | Top! Ik kom bij jou langs. Ik neem koekjes mee. | Great! I'll come round to yours. I'll bring biscuits. |
| Sanne | Gezellig! Ik zet de koffie klaar. 😊 | Lovely! I'll have the coffee ready. |

**Check questions:**

1. What day is Grace's appointment? (a) Tuesday (b) Thursday (c) Friday. **Answer: b**
2. What time? (a) 10:00 (b) 14:30 (c) 15:30. **Answer: b**
3. Why does Sanne cancel? (a) she has to work (b) her daughter is ill (c) she's on holiday. **Answer: b**
4. Why can't Kofi meet on Monday? (a) he works until six (b) he has a class (c) he's ill. **Answer: a**
5. What will Kofi bring? (a) coffee (b) flowers (c) biscuits. **Answer: c**

## Part 3: Grammar

### 3A. Ordinal numbers: eerste, tweede, derde

| Rule | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| 1, 3 and 8 are special | **eerste** (1st), **derde** (3rd), **achtste** (8th) |
| 2 to 19: add **-de** | tweede, vierde, vijfde, zesde, zevende, negende, tiende, elfde, twaalfde, dertiende, negentiende |
| 20 and up: add **-ste** | twintigste, eenentwintigste, dertigste, honderdste |

Written short: **1e, 2e, 3e, 20e** (or 1ste, 2de, 3de).

### 3B. Dates and days

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Welke dag is het vandaag? | Het is dinsdag. |
| Wat is de datum vandaag? | Het is 8 november. (said: acht november) |
| Wanneer ben je jarig? | Ik ben op 3 mei jarig. |
| Wanneer is de afspraak? | Op donderdag 12 juni om half drie. |

**Rules:**

1. **Normal numbers** in written dates: **8 november**, said "acht november". With a weekday you'll often hear the ordinal: **donderdag de twaalfde**.
2. **Order:** day number, month, year: **12-06-2026** is 12 June, not 6 December.
3. **Prepositions:**

| Preposition | Use it with | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| op | days and dates | **op** maandag, **op** 3 mei |
| in | months, seasons, years | **in** juni, **in** de zomer, **in** 2026 |
| om | clock times | **om** half drie |
| van ... tot | from ... until | **van** negen **tot** vijf |
| over | in (time from now) | **over** twee weken |

**Watch out:** English "in two weeks" is **over** twee weken, not "in twee weken". And "on Monday" can also be just **maandag**: Maandag werk ik.

**Habits:** add **-s** for "every": **'s maandags** (on Mondays), **'s zomers** (in summer). You'll also hear **op maandag** or **elke maandag** with the same meaning.

### 3C. Separable verbs

Many Dutch verbs are made of a **prefix** plus a verb: **op**+staan, **mee**+nemen, **af**+spreken. In a normal sentence, the verb part goes in position 2 and the prefix goes to the **very end**.

| 1 | Verb (2) | Middle | Prefix (end) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ik | sta | om zeven uur | op. |
| Om zeven uur | sta | ik | op. |
| Hij | neemt | koekjes | mee. |
| De trein | komt | om tien uur | aan. |
| Wij | nodigen | onze buren | uit. |

**Questions:** the verb part comes first, the prefix still goes to the end: **Kom** je morgen **langs**? **Neem** jij de koffie **mee**?

**With a modal verb** (A1.6): the separable verb stays in one piece at the end, as an infinitive: Ik **moet** om zeven uur **opstaan**. Kun je de koffie **meenemen**?

**How do you know a verb is separable?** In the dictionary, separable verbs are marked with a mark like op|staan. The common prefixes are: **aan, af, bij, in, mee, na, op, terug, uit, voor, weg, langs, klaar, thuis**. These prefixes are also words on their own, and they always carry the stress.

### 3D. Inseparable verbs

Verbs that start with **be-, ge-, her-, ont-, ver-** or **er-** never split:

- Ik **begin** om negen uur. (beginnen)
- De trein **vertrekt** om tien uur. (vertrekken)
- Ik **betaal** met pin. (betalen)
- Hij **herhaalt** de zin. (herhalen, to repeat)
- Ik **ontbijt** om zeven uur. (ontbijten)

These prefixes are not words on their own, and they are **never** stressed.

### Typical mistakes for English speakers

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ik opsta om zeven uur. | Ik sta om zeven uur op. | Prefix goes to the end |
| Ik sta op om zeven uur. | Ik sta om zeven uur op. | The prefix is the last word |
| Ik moet sta op. | Ik moet opstaan. | After a modal: one infinitive |
| Ik be gin om negen uur. | Ik begin om negen uur. | be- verbs never split |
| op juni | in juni | Months take in |
| in maandag | op maandag | Days take op |
| in twee weken | over twee weken | "in two weeks' time" = over |
| Maandag 3 Maart | maandag 3 maart | No capitals for days and months |

## Part 4: Pronunciation

### 4A. Stress: separable vs inseparable

| Type | Stress | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Separable | on the **prefix** | **OP**staan, **MEE**nemen, **AF**spreken, **LANGS**komen, te**RUG**bellen, **UIT**nodigen |
| Inseparable | on the **verb**, never on be-/ver-/ont- | be**GIN**nen, ver**TREK**ken, be**TA**len, ont**BIJ**ten, her**HA**len |

This is a useful rule in both directions. If you hear the stress on the first part, the verb probably splits. When you speak, stress the prefix even when it stands alone at the end: Ik sta om zeven uur **OP**.

**Note:** terugbellen is stressed **te-RUG**-bellen in careful speech, because terug itself is stressed on rug. The whole prefix **terug** still carries the main stress of the verb.

## Part 8: Culture & review

### Time the Dutch way

- **The agenda rules:** the Dutch plan ahead, even for friends. "Even in mijn agenda kijken" is normal, and a dinner can be planned weeks in advance. Turning up unannounced is unusual, except with close family.
- **Punctuality:** be on time for appointments. For a birthday at home, ten minutes late is fine.
- **Cancelling:** always cancel (afzeggen) as early as you can. Many doctors, dentists and hairdressers charge you if you don't come and don't cancel at least 24 hours before.
- **Week numbers:** at work and school people often say "in week 45" instead of a date. Your phone calendar can show week numbers.
- **Holidays:** Koningsdag (27 April), Bevrijdingsdag (5 May), and Christian holidays like Hemelvaart, Pasen and Pinksteren. School holidays are spread over three regions (north, middle, south), so not everybody has the same weeks off.
- **Flemish note:** in Belgium you'll hear **de voormiddag** (morning) and **de namiddag** (afternoon), and times are often written **10u30**. Belgium's national holiday is 21 July, and the Flemish community celebrates 11 July.
