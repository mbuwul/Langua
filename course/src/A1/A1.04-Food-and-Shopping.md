# A1.4 Food & Shopping (Boodschappen doen)

## Unit at a glance

After this unit (about 10 hours) you can buy food at a bakery, a market and a supermarket in Dutch. That means asking questions, saying what you want or don't want, understanding prices, and paying.

**You will be able to:**

- Greet and take your turn in a shop (Wie is er aan de beurt?)
- Ask for products and quantities (Ik wil graag een pond kaas.)
- Ask yes/no questions and question-word questions (Heeft u brood? Wat kost dat?)
- Say you don't have or don't want something with **geen** and **niet**
- Understand prices said aloud (twee euro vijfenzeventig) and in writing (€2,75)
- Read a supermarket offer leaflet and write a shopping message

**Builds on:** A0.4 (niet and geen), A0.5 (questions, prices), A1.1 to A1.3.

## Part 1: Situation

You hear each dialogue three times: at natural speed without text, then slower with the Dutch text, then with the English. Afterwards you answer three quick questions to show you understood the main points.

### Dialogue 1: Bij de bakker (At the bakery)

Anna uses **u** (formal "you") with the baker. That's normal with shop staff you don't know.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bakker | Goedemorgen! Wie is er aan de beurt? | Good morning! Who's next? |
| Anna | Ik. Goedemorgen. Heeft u volkorenbrood? | Me. Good morning. Do you have wholemeal bread? |
| Bakker | Ja, natuurlijk. Wilt u een heel of een half brood? | Yes, of course. Would you like a whole or a half loaf? |
| Anna | Een half, graag. Is het gesneden? | A half, please. Is it sliced? |
| Bakker | Ik snijd het even voor u. Verder nog iets? | I'll slice it for you. Anything else? |
| Anna | Ja, vier krentenbollen, alstublieft. | Yes, four currant buns, please. |
| Bakker | Sorry, ik heb geen krentenbollen meer. Ik heb wel croissants. | Sorry, I have no currant buns left. I do have croissants. |
| Anna | Nee, dank u. Wat kost het? | No, thank you. How much is it? |
| Bakker | Dat is dan twee euro vijfenzeventig. | That's two euros seventy-five, then. |
| Anna | Kan ik pinnen? | Can I pay by card? |
| Bakker | Ja hoor. Alstublieft, uw bon. | Sure. Here you are, your receipt. |
| Anna | Dank u wel. Fijne dag! | Thank you. Have a nice day! |
| Bakker | Insgelijks! | Likewise! |

### Dialogue 2: Op de markt (At the market)

At a market stall people often use **je** (informal "you"). Mark and the seller are relaxed and friendly.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verkoper | Zeg het maar! | What can I get you? (literally: Just say it!) |
| Mark | Hoeveel kosten de appels? | How much are the apples? |
| Verkoper | Twee euro per kilo. | Two euros a kilo. |
| Mark | Zijn ze zoet? | Are they sweet? |
| Verkoper | Ja, heel zoet. Wil je proeven? | Yes, very sweet. Do you want a taste? |
| Mark | Lekker! Doe maar een kilo. En heb je ook aardbeien? | Tasty! I'll take a kilo. And do you also have strawberries? |
| Verkoper | Nee, het is geen seizoen. | No, they're not in season. |
| Mark | Jammer. Dan neem ik een bakje tomaten en een pond uien. | Pity. Then I'll take a punnet of tomatoes and half a kilo of onions. |
| Verkoper | Anders nog iets? | Anything else? |
| Mark | Nee, dat was het. | No, that's all. |
| Verkoper | Dat wordt dan vier euro vijftig. | That comes to four euros fifty. |
| Mark | Alsjeblieft. | Here you are. |
| Verkoper | Dank je wel. Tot de volgende keer! | Thanks. See you next time! |

**Notice:** Anna says **alstublieft** (formal), and Mark says **alsjeblieft** (informal). Both mean "please" and "here you are".

**Check questions (after first listening):**

1. What does Anna buy? (a) a whole loaf (b) half a loaf and croissants (c) half a loaf. **Answer: c**
2. Why doesn't Mark buy strawberries? (a) too expensive (b) not in season (c) not sweet. **Answer: b**
3. How does Anna pay? (a) cash (b) by card (c) by phone. **Answer: b**

### Prices

Dutch writes a comma where English writes a point: **€2,75**. Say the euros, then "euro", then the cents: **twee euro vijfenzeventig**. "Euro" never becomes "euros". The cents are usually said without the word "cent".

## Part 3: Grammar

**Remember:** yes/no questions and question words were taught in A0.5, and niet vs geen in A0.4. **New in this unit:** shop questions with **welk/welke**, and where **niet** goes in longer sentences with objects.

This unit covers two points. First, how to ask questions. Second, how to say "not" with **niet** and **geen**. In the app the verb is shown in **blue** and the subject in **green**, so you can see the word order move.

### 3A. Yes/no questions: put the verb first

English needs "do" to make a question ("Do you have bread?"). Dutch doesn't use "do". You just move the verb to the front.

| Statement | Question | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| U **heeft** brood. | **Heeft** u brood? | Do you have bread? |
| De appels **zijn** zoet. | **Zijn** de appels zoet? | Are the apples sweet? |
| Jullie **kopen** kaas. | **Kopen** jullie kaas? | Are you (all) buying cheese? |

**The jij/je rule:** when **jij** or **je** comes straight after the verb, the verb loses its **-t**. This only happens with jij/je, not with u or other subjects.

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Jij koop**t** brood. | Koop jij brood? |
| Je heb**t** een tas. | Heb je een tas? |
| Jij wil**t** proeven. | Wil jij proeven? |
| Jij ben**t** aan de beurt. | Ben jij aan de beurt? |
| U heef**t** een tas. | Heef**t** u een tas? (keeps -t) |

### 3B. Question words: question word + verb + subject

The question word comes first and the verb comes second, followed by the subject.

| Question word | English | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wat | what | Wat kost de kaas? |
| wie | who | Wie is er aan de beurt? |
| waar | where | Waar is de kassa? |
| wanneer | when | Wanneer is de markt? |
| hoeveel | how much, how many | Hoeveel appels wil je? |
| hoe | how | Hoe betaalt u? |
| welk / welke | which | Welk brood neemt u? Welke kaas is lekker? |

**welk or welke?** Use **welk** with het-words in the singular (welk brood). Use **welke** with de-words and all plurals (welke kaas, welke broden).

### 3C. Niet or geen?

Both mean "not" or "no". The choice depends on the noun.

**Use geen** with a noun that has **een** or no article. Geen replaces the een.

- Ik heb **een** tas. → Ik heb **geen** tas. (I don't have a bag.)
- Ik koop brood. → Ik koop **geen** brood. (I'm not buying any bread.)
- Wij hebben aardbeien. → Wij hebben **geen** aardbeien. (We have no strawberries.)

**Use niet** for everything else: a noun with **de/het/mijn/dit**, a verb, a describing word or a name.

- Ik koop **de** kaas **niet**. (I'm not buying the cheese.)
- Anna pint **niet**. (Anna isn't paying by card.)
- De appels zijn **niet** duur. (The apples aren't expensive.)

**Where does niet go?** At A1 you need three rules:

1. At the end of a short sentence: Ik koop het brood **niet**.
2. Before a describing word: De vis is **niet** vers.
3. Before a place with a preposition, or before a second verb: Ik ga **niet** naar de markt. Ik wil **niet** pinnen.

### Typical mistakes for English speakers

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doe je hebben brood? | Heb je brood? | Dutch has no "do" in questions |
| Hebt je brood? | Heb je brood? | Drop -t before je/jij |
| Wat de kaas kost? | Wat kost de kaas? | Verb comes second, after the question word |
| Ik heb niet een tas. | Ik heb geen tas. | een + noun → geen |
| Ik niet koop de kaas. | Ik koop de kaas niet. | niet never goes between subject and verb |

## Part 4: Pronunciation

The main focus is **question intonation**. The unit also revisits two sounds English speakers find hard: **ui** (as in de ui, onion) and **g** (as in graag). For every item you listen to the model, record yourself, and get a score from 0 to 100 for each sound and for the melody. A score of 70 or more counts as a pass.

### 4A. Question melody

| Sentence type | Melody | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statement | Falls at the end ↘ | U heeft brood. ↘ |
| Yes/no question | Rises at the end ↗ | Heeft u brood? ↗ |
| Question-word question | Usually falls ↘ (a small rise sounds extra friendly) | Wat kost dat? ↘ |

**Exercise 4A.** Listen and decide: is it a statement or a question? Then repeat it with the same melody.

1. Je hebt kaas. / Heb je kaas?
2. Het brood is vers. / Is het brood vers?
3. U pint. / Pint u?
4. Hoeveel kost het?

### 4B. The sound ui

There is no ui in English. To make it, start with the vowel in English "hut", round your lips tightly, then glide towards the "u" in French "tu". It's **not** the "ow" in "how".

Practice words: de **ui**, de **ui**en, het fr**ui**t, b**ui**ten (outside), het h**ui**s (house), de kr**ui**den (herbs).

**Contrast drill.** Hear and repeat the three sounds that learners mix up:

| ui | ij / ei | ou / au |
| --- | --- | --- |
| de ui (onion) | het ijs (ice cream) | oud (old) |
| het huis (house) | het ei (egg) | het goud (gold) |
| buiten (outside) | de wijn (wine) | koud (cold) |

### 4C. The g and ch

In Standard Netherlands Dutch, **g** and **ch** are both a rough sound at the back of the throat, like the "ch" in Scottish "loch". In the south and in Belgium it's softer. Both are correct; the course models the Netherlands sound. Practice words: **g**raa**g**, **g**oedemor**g**en, **g**oedkoop, lekker (no g!), a**ch**t, to**ch**.

### 4D. Long and short a

**aa** is long and open, like "a" in "father". Single **a** in a closed syllable is short, like "a" in British "bath" said quickly. Contrast: de k**aa**s (cheese) vs de k**a**ssa (checkout); het p**a**k (carton) vs het p**aa**r (pair).

**Final task:** read Dialogue 1 aloud as Anna. Your recording is scored on ui, g, aa/a and question melody.

## Part 5: Listening & reading

The tasks use the same format as the integration and state exams: a short real-life text, then multiple-choice questions. You get a time limit, the way you would in the exam.

### 5A. Reading: supermarket leaflet (time: 8 minutes)

> **AANBIEDINGEN DEZE WEEK** (maandag t/m zaterdag)
>
> - Appels, 1 kilo: €1,99
> - Jonge kaas, 500 gram: €4,49 — **2e halve prijs**
> - Melk, 1 liter: €0,99
> - Volkorenbrood, heel: €2,29
> - Koffie, 250 gram: €3,79 — **1 + 1 gratis**
>
> **Openingstijden:** ma t/m za 08.00–21.00 uur, zo 12.00–18.00 uur
>
> Betalen? Wij accepteren alleen **pin**.

**Useful:** t/m = tot en met (up to and including). gratis = free. ma, di, wo, do, vr, za, zo = Monday to Sunday.

2. Je koopt twee pakken koffie. Hoeveel betaal je? (a) €3,79 (b) €5,69 (c) €7,58. **Answer: a** (1 + 1 gratis = the second one is free)
3. What does "2e halve prijs" mean? (a) everything is half price (b) the second one is half price (c) two for the price of one. **Answer: b**
4. Kun je op zondag om 10.00 uur boodschappen doen? (a) ja (b) nee. **Answer: b** (Sunday opens at 12.00)
5. Op welke dag is de laatste dag van de aanbieding? (a) vrijdag (b) zaterdag (c) zondag. **Answer: b**
6. Hoe kun je betalen? (a) alleen contant (b) alleen met pin (c) contant en met pin. **Answer: b**

## Part 8: Culture & review

### Boodschappen in Nederland (shopping in the Netherlands)

- **Pinnen is normal.** Many shops and market stalls prefer cards, and some accept only card ("Wij accepteren alleen pin"). Contactless payment by phone is common.
- **Bring your own bag.** Plastic bags cost money, so most people carry a tas or a fietstas (bike bag).
- **Wait for your turn.** At the bakery, butcher or cheese shop, staff ask "Wie is er aan de beurt?" and expect you to say "Ik" when it's your turn. Some shops use a numbered ticket (een nummertje trekken).
- **Bread for lunch.** A typical Dutch lunch is a few slices of bread (boterhammen) with cheese, ham or **hagelslag**. Hot meals are usually eaten in the evening, around 18.00.
- **The weekly market.** Most towns have a market once or twice a week. It's cheaper for fruit and vegetables, and the sellers are more informal (je instead of u).
