# A1.5 Home & City (Huis en stad)

## Unit at a glance

After this unit (about 9 hours) you can describe your home and your neighbourhood, say where things are, and ask for and follow simple directions in town. You'll learn **er is / er zijn** (there is / there are) and the prepositions of place (in, op, onder, naast, tussen...). In pronunciation you train the hardest vowel pair for English speakers: **ij** and **ui**.

**You will be able to:**

- Name the rooms, furniture and places in a Dutch home and town (about 60 words)
- Say what there is and isn't: Er is een tuin. Er zijn twee slaapkamers. Er is geen lift.
- Say exactly where something is with 12 prepositions of place
- Ask the way and understand directions: rechtdoor, linksaf, de tweede straat rechts
- Hear and say the difference between ij and ui (bij / bui, tijd / tuit)
- Understand Dutch floor numbers and addresses

**Builds on:** A1.2 (de/het, plurals), A1.3 (present tense, verb second), A1.4 (questions, niet and geen).

## Part 1: Situation

### Dialogue 1: Het nieuwe huis (The new house)

Grace and her family have moved. Her friend Fatima comes to see the house. They use **je**.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fatima | Wat een mooi huis! Hoeveel kamers zijn er? | What a lovely house! How many rooms are there? |
| Grace | Er zijn vijf kamers. Beneden is de woonkamer en de keuken. | There are five rooms. Downstairs is the living room and the kitchen. |
| Fatima | De keuken is groot! Is er ook een tuin? | The kitchen is big! Is there a garden too? |
| Grace | Ja, er is een kleine tuin achter het huis. | Yes, there's a small garden behind the house. |
| Fatima | En boven? | And upstairs? |
| Grace | Boven zijn er drie slaapkamers en de badkamer. Leo heeft een kamer naast onze slaapkamer. | Upstairs there are three bedrooms and the bathroom. Leo has a room next to our bedroom. |
| Fatima | En de derde slaapkamer? | And the third bedroom? |
| Grace | Dat is mijn werkkamer. Mijn bureau staat voor het raam. | That's my study. My desk is in front of the window. |
| Fatima | Heerlijk. En de buurt? | Lovely. And the neighbourhood? |
| Grace | Ook goed. Er is een supermarkt op de hoek en de school is tegenover het park. | Good too. There's a supermarket on the corner and the school is opposite the park. |

**Note:** "een kleine tuin" has an -e on klein. You'll learn when that -e appears in A1.8. For now, learn it as a phrase.

### Dialogue 2: Waar is de bibliotheek? (Where is the library?)

Mei is in the centre of Utrecht and asks an older man the way. She uses **u**.

| Who | Dutch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mei | Pardon, meneer. Weet u waar de bibliotheek is? | Excuse me, sir. Do you know where the library is? |
| Meneer | De bibliotheek? Ja. U gaat hier rechtdoor tot het plein. | The library? Yes. You go straight on here as far as the square. |
| Mei | Tot het plein. Ja. | As far as the square. Yes. |
| Meneer | Bij het plein gaat u linksaf. Dan neemt u de tweede straat rechts. | At the square you turn left. Then you take the second street on the right. |
| Mei | De tweede straat rechts. | The second street on the right. |
| Meneer | De bibliotheek is aan de linkerkant, naast een café. | The library is on the left-hand side, next to a café. |
| Mei | Is het ver? | Is it far? |
| Meneer | Nee hoor, het is vijf minuten lopen. | No, not at all, it's a five-minute walk. |
| Mei | Dank u wel! | Thank you very much! |
| Meneer | Graag gedaan. | You're welcome. |

**Check questions:**

1. How many bedrooms does Grace's house have? (a) two (b) three (c) five. **Answer: b**
2. Where is the garden? (a) in front of the house (b) behind the house (c) on the roof. **Answer: b**
3. What is the third bedroom used for? (a) a guest room (b) a study (c) Leo's room. **Answer: b**
4. Which way does Mei turn at the square? (a) left (b) right (c) straight on. **Answer: a**
5. What is next to the library? (a) a café (b) a park (c) a school. **Answer: a**

## Part 3: Grammar

### 3A. er is / er zijn (there is / there are)

Use **er is** with one thing and **er zijn** with more than one.

| | Statement | Question | Negative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One | Er is een tuin. | Is er een tuin? | Er is geen tuin. |
| More | Er zijn drie kamers. | Zijn er drie kamers? | Er zijn geen kamers vrij. |

**Verb second still applies.** When you start with a place or time, **er** moves behind the verb:

- **Er is** een supermarkt in de straat.
- In de straat **is er** een supermarkt.
- Boven **zijn er** drie slaapkamers.

**Use geen, not niet, with "een" and with plurals** (from A1.4): Er is **geen** lift. Er zijn **geen** winkels.

**Hoeveel ... zijn er?** (How many ... are there?) Hoeveel slaapkamers zijn er?

### 3B. Prepositions of place

| Dutch | English | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| in | in | De sleutel is **in** de kast. |
| op | on (on top of) | Het boek ligt **op** de tafel. |
| aan | on (attached to) | Het schilderij hangt **aan** de muur. |
| onder | under | De kat slaapt **onder** het bed. |
| boven | above | De lamp hangt **boven** de tafel. |
| naast | next to | De bank staat **naast** de kast. |
| tussen | between | De stoel staat **tussen** de tafel en het raam. |
| voor | in front of | Mijn fiets staat **voor** het huis. |
| achter | behind | De tuin is **achter** het huis. |
| tegenover | opposite | De school is **tegenover** het park. |
| bij | at, near | Ik woon **bij** het station. |
| op de hoek | on the corner | De bakker is **op de hoek**. |

**Two traps for English speakers:**

1. **op** vs **aan**. Things lying on a flat surface are **op** (op de tafel). Things hanging on a wall or a door are **aan** (aan de muur).
2. Floors and streets use **op** and **in**: **op** de eerste verdieping (on the first floor), **in** de Lindelaan (in/on Linden Avenue). English "on the street" is **in** de straat or **op** straat (outdoors).

### 3C. staan, liggen, hangen, zitten

English often says "is" for where something is. Dutch usually says **how** it is there:

| Verb | Use it for | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| staan | upright things: furniture, bottles, bikes, buildings | De tafel **staat** in de keuken. |
| liggen | flat or lying things: books, phones, keys, people in bed | Mijn telefoon **ligt** op de bank. |
| hangen | things hanging: pictures, lamps, coats | De jas **hangt** aan de deur. |
| zitten | things inside something closed, people on chairs | De sleutel **zit** in mijn tas. |

**zijn** is never wrong: "De tafel is in de keuken" is fine. But staan, liggen and hangen sound much more natural, and you'll hear them all the time.

### 3D. Asking where: waar

- **Waar is** de wc? (Where's the toilet?)
- **Waar woon je?** (Where do you live?)
- **Weet u waar** het station **is**? (Do you know where the station is?)

**Notice:** in the last example, the verb **is** goes to the end after "waar". This is called a subordinate clause, and you'll learn the rule in A2. For now, learn **Weet u waar ... is?** as a fixed, polite phrase.

### Typical mistakes for English speakers

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Het is een tuin. | Er is een tuin. | "There is" = er is |
| Er is drie kamers. | Er zijn drie kamers. | Plural → er zijn |
| Boven er zijn twee kamers. | Boven zijn er twee kamers. | Verb second |
| Er is niet een lift. | Er is geen lift. | geen with een-words |
| Het schilderij is op de muur. | Het schilderij hangt aan de muur. | Hanging = aan |
| Ik woon op de Lindelaan. | Ik woon in de Lindelaan. | Streets take in |
| Het boek staat op de tafel. | Het boek ligt op de tafel. | A flat book lies |

## Part 4: Pronunciation

### 4A. ij/ei and ui

You met these sounds in A0.3. Now you'll train them against each other, because mixing them up changes the meaning.

| Sound | Spelling | How to make it | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ij / ei | ij, ei (same sound) | Start with the e of "bed", glide towards "ee". Lips spread, not rounded. Not "eye"! | tijd, mijn, wijk, klein, trein |
| ui | ui | Start with a rounded "uh" (lips like a kiss), glide forward. No English equivalent. | huis, tuin, buiten, ruim, duim |

**Careful:** buurt has **uu**, not ui. That's a different, single vowel (A0.1).

**The two classic mistakes:**

1. Saying **ij** like English "eye" (Dutch mijn ≠ English "mine"). The Dutch sound starts higher, from e.
2. Saying **ui** like "ow" or like ij. Keep your lips rounded for the whole sound.

### 4B. Minimal pairs: ij vs ui

| Sound 1 | Sound 2 | Meanings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bij | bui | at / rain shower |
| rijk | ruik | rich / (I) smell |
| tijd | tuit | time / spout |
| spijt | spuit | regret / syringe, spray |
| mijl | muil | mile / slipper |
| lijk | luik | corpse / hatch, shutter |
| bijt | buit | bites / loot |

## Part 8: Culture & review

### Dutch homes and towns

- **No curtains:** in many Dutch streets you can look straight into people's living rooms. Leaving the curtains open is normal and says "we have nothing to hide". Staring in is still rude!
- **Steep stairs:** older Dutch houses have very steep, narrow stairs. Moving furniture often goes through the window, using the hook (hijsbalk) at the top of old canal houses.
- **Floor numbers:** the ground floor is **de begane grond**. **De eerste verdieping** is one floor up, as in British English.
- **Addresses:** the street comes first, then the number: **Lindelaan 8**. A postcode has four numbers and two letters: **3521 AB**. House numbers can have letters (12a, 12b) for flats in one building.
- **Registration:** when you move, you must register at the **gemeente** (municipality). You then get a **BSN**, the personal number you need for work, the doctor and the bank.
- **Bikes:** there are more bikes than people in the Netherlands. Every station has a huge **fietsenstalling** (bike park).
- **Flemish note:** in Belgium the ground floor is **het gelijkvloers**, the living room is often **de living**, and a flat in a block is **een appartement**. You'll also hear **de koer** for a small yard.
